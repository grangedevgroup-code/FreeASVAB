# Auto Information {data-sub="How engines and vehicle systems work — and how to diagnose the most common problems"}

Auto Information (AI) tests your knowledge of how vehicles work and how they are maintained and repaired. On the computer test you get **10 questions in 7 minutes**. Your Auto and Shop results are combined into one score called **AS**, which feeds the Army's **CO, GM, MM, OF,** and **SC** line scores, the Air Force **Mechanical** composite, and mechanical jobs in every branch.

You do not need to be a mechanic. Most questions ask what a part **does**, which system it belongs to, or what is **most likely wrong** when a vehicle shows a certain symptom. Learn the job of each part and the troubleshooting table at the end of this chapter, and you will be ready.

## How an Engine Makes Power: The Four-Stroke Cycle

Most car engines are **four-stroke internal combustion engines**. Inside each **cylinder**, a **piston** slides up and down. Every power cycle takes four strokes of the piston:

{{fig:ai-four-stroke|The four-stroke cycle}}

| Stroke | Valves | Piston | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Intake** | Intake valve **open** | Moves **down** | The air–fuel mixture is drawn into the cylinder |
| **2. Compression** | **Both closed** | Moves **up** | The mixture is squeezed into a small space |
| **3. Power** (combustion) | **Both closed** | Pushed **down** | The spark plug fires; the burning mixture expands and forces the piston down — the **only stroke that makes power** |
| **4. Exhaust** | Exhaust valve **open** | Moves **up** | Burned gases are pushed out of the cylinder |
{.compact}

::: memory Suck, squeeze, bang, blow
**Intake** (suck), **compression** (squeeze), **power** (bang), **exhaust** (blow). One complete cycle takes **two turns of the crankshaft**.
:::

### Engine parts

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| **Engine block** | The main body of the engine; contains the cylinders |
| **Piston** | Moves up and down in the cylinder; **piston rings** seal it against the cylinder wall |
| **Connecting rod** | Links the piston to the crankshaft |
| **Crankshaft** | Turns the pistons' up-and-down motion into **rotating** motion |
| **Camshaft** | Its lobes open and close the **valves** at the right time; it turns at **half** the crankshaft's speed |
| **Timing belt or chain** | Keeps the crankshaft and camshaft in step |
| **Valves** (intake and exhaust) | Let the mixture in and the exhaust out; valve springs close them |
| **Cylinder head** | Caps the cylinders; holds the valves and spark plugs. The **head gasket** seals it to the block |
| **Flywheel** | A heavy wheel on the crankshaft that smooths out rotation; the starter turns it to start the engine |
| **Oil pan** | Holds the engine oil at the bottom of the engine |
| **Intake and exhaust manifolds** | Carry air into the cylinders and exhaust out of them |
| **Turbocharger** | Uses exhaust gas to spin a fan that forces extra air into the engine (a **supercharger** does the same job but is driven by a belt) |
{.compact}

### Gasoline vs. diesel engines

- **Gasoline engines** use **spark plugs** to ignite the air–fuel mixture (spark ignition).
- **Diesel engines** have **no spark plugs**. They compress air so tightly that it becomes **hot enough to ignite the fuel** when it is injected (compression ignition). **Glow plugs** warm the cylinders to help a cold diesel start. Diesels produce more torque and usually get better fuel economy.
- **Two-stroke engines** (chain saws, some small outboard motors) complete a power cycle in one turn of the crankshaft and usually burn oil mixed into the fuel.
- **Displacement** is the total volume swept by all the pistons, measured in liters or cubic inches. Engines are often arranged **inline** (in a row) or in a **V** shape (V6, V8).

## The Fuel and Air System

- **Fuel path:** fuel tank → **fuel pump** → **fuel filter** → **fuel injectors**, which spray a fine mist of fuel into the engine. An **engine control unit (ECU)** — the car's computer — times the injectors using information from sensors.
- **Older vehicles** use a **carburetor** to mix air and fuel. A **choke** enriches the mixture (more fuel) for cold starts.
- **Air path:** the **air filter** cleans incoming air; the **throttle** (controlled by the gas pedal) controls how much air enters.
- A **rich** mixture has too much fuel (black smoke, poor mileage). A **lean** mixture has too little fuel (the engine can run hot and ping).
- **Octane** measures a fuel's resistance to **knocking** (also called pinging or detonation) — burning too early, before the spark. Higher-octane fuel resists knock; it does not contain "more power."

## The Ignition System

Gasoline engines need a strong spark at exactly the right moment.

- **Battery** (12 volts) → **ignition switch** → **ignition coil**, which steps the voltage up to **thousands of volts** (often 20,000 or more) → the spark travels to the **spark plug**, where it jumps the **gap** between two electrodes.
- Older vehicles use a **distributor** (with a cap and rotor) to send the spark to each cylinder in the right **firing order**. Most modern engines use a separate coil for each plug ("coil-on-plug").
- **Ignition timing** is when the spark fires, usually just before the piston reaches the top of its compression stroke.
- The spark plug gap is measured with a **feeler gauge** or wire gauge.

## The Starting and Charging System

- **Battery:** a 12-volt **lead–acid** battery made of six cells (about 12.6 volts when fully charged). Its electrolyte is sulfuric acid and water. The **positive** post is usually marked **+** and red; the **negative** post **−** and black.
- **Starter motor:** turns the engine over (cranks it) to start it. The **starter solenoid** is a heavy-duty switch that sends battery current to the starter and pushes the starter gear into the flywheel.
- **Alternator:** driven by a belt from the engine, it **recharges the battery** and powers the electrical system **while the engine runs**. It produces AC, which built-in diodes convert to DC. A **voltage regulator** keeps its output steady (about 13.5 to 14.5 volts).
- **Fuses** protect each circuit from too much current.

::: trap Battery safety
Batteries give off explosive hydrogen gas. When **removing** a battery, disconnect the **negative** cable **first**; when installing, connect it **last**. When **jump-starting**, make the final (negative) connection on bare metal on the engine of the dead vehicle, away from the battery.
:::

## The Cooling System

An engine produces far more heat than it can use. The cooling system carries that heat away.

- **Coolant** (antifreeze — usually a mix of ethylene glycol and water, often 50/50) flows through passages in the engine called **water jackets**, picking up heat.
- The belt-driven **water pump** circulates the coolant.
- The **thermostat** is a valve that stays **closed** while the engine is cold (so it warms up quickly) and **opens** when the engine reaches operating temperature, letting coolant flow to the radiator.
- The **radiator** passes hot coolant through thin tubes and fins so air can carry the heat away; a **fan** pulls air through it when the car is slow or stopped.
- The **radiator cap** keeps the system under pressure, which raises the coolant's boiling point. **Never open it on a hot engine.**
- The **heater core** is a small radiator that heats the passenger cabin.

::: key What makes an engine overheat
Low coolant or a leak, a thermostat **stuck closed**, a failed **water pump**, a broken or slipping **belt**, a clogged **radiator**, a failed **fan**, or a blown **head gasket**.
:::

## The Lubrication System

- **Engine oil** reduces friction and wear, carries heat away, cleans, and helps seal the piston rings.
- The **oil pump** draws oil from the **oil pan** and pushes it through the **oil filter** to the bearings and other moving parts.
- Check the oil level with the **dipstick** (engine off, car on level ground).
- **Viscosity** is a measure of how thick an oil is. In a rating such as **10W-30**, the **W stands for winter**: the first number shows how easily the oil flows when cold (lower is thinner), and the second shows its thickness at operating temperature.
- If the **oil pressure warning light** comes on while driving, stop the engine as soon as it is safe — running without oil pressure can destroy an engine in minutes.

## Exhaust and Emission Controls

- **Exhaust path:** exhaust manifold → **oxygen sensor** → **catalytic converter** → **muffler** → tailpipe.
- The **catalytic converter** changes harmful gases (carbon monoxide, unburned fuel, and nitrogen oxides) into less harmful ones (carbon dioxide, water, and nitrogen).
- The **muffler** reduces exhaust **noise**.
- The **oxygen sensor** measures oxygen in the exhaust so the computer can adjust the air–fuel mixture.
- The **PCV valve** (positive crankcase ventilation) routes gases that leak past the piston rings back into the intake to be burned.
- The **EGR valve** (exhaust gas recirculation) sends a little exhaust back into the cylinders to lower combustion temperature and reduce nitrogen oxides.
- The **EVAP system** traps fuel vapors in a charcoal canister.
- Since 1996, U.S. cars have **OBD-II** on-board diagnostics. A lit **check engine light** means a trouble code is stored; a scan tool reads it.

::: trap Carbon monoxide
Exhaust contains **carbon monoxide**, a colorless, odorless, deadly gas. Never run an engine in a closed garage or other enclosed space.
:::

## The Drivetrain

The drivetrain carries the engine's power to the wheels.

{{fig:ai-drivetrain|Rear-wheel-drive drivetrain: how power reaches the wheels}}

- The **transmission** uses gears to trade speed for **torque** (turning force). **Low gears** give lots of torque for starting and climbing; **high gears** give speed and fuel economy at highway speeds.
- In a **manual transmission**, the **clutch** disconnects the engine from the transmission so you can change gears. Its main parts are the **clutch disc**, **pressure plate**, and **flywheel**. A **slipping clutch** (engine speed rises but the car does not speed up) usually means a worn clutch disc.
- An **automatic transmission** uses a **torque converter** (a fluid coupling) instead of a clutch, plus planetary gear sets. Automatic transmission fluid is usually **red**.
- **Rear-wheel drive:** the **driveshaft** (with **universal joints**, or U-joints, at each end) carries power to the **differential** in the rear axle.
- **Front-wheel drive:** the transmission and differential are combined in a **transaxle**, and **CV (constant velocity) joints** on the axle shafts let the front wheels steer and receive power at the same time. A clicking noise while turning often means a worn CV joint.
- The **differential** lets the drive wheels turn at **different speeds** — necessary in a turn, when the outside wheel must travel farther than the inside wheel.
- **Four-wheel-drive** vehicles use a **transfer case** to send power to both the front and rear axles.

## Brakes

Car brakes are **hydraulic**: pressing the pedal pushes a piston in the **master cylinder**, which forces **brake fluid** through the **brake lines** to each wheel.

| | Disc brakes | Drum brakes |
|---|---|---|
| Parts | **Caliper** squeezes **brake pads** against a spinning **rotor** (disc) | **Wheel cylinder** pushes **brake shoes** outward against a spinning **drum** |
| Where found | Front wheels of almost every car (the front brakes do most of the stopping); often the rear too | Rear wheels of some cars and trucks |
{.compact}

- A **power brake booster** uses engine vacuum to reduce the force you need on the pedal.
- **ABS** (anti-lock braking system) pumps the brakes automatically during hard stops so the wheels do not lock — which lets you keep **steering** while braking.
- The **parking brake** is mechanical (cable-operated), so it works even if the hydraulic system fails.
- **Brake fluid** absorbs moisture over time and must be replaced periodically.

## Suspension, Steering, Wheels, and Tires

- **Springs** (coil springs, leaf springs, or torsion bars) hold up the vehicle's weight and absorb bumps.
- **Shock absorbers** (dampers) stop the springs from bouncing. A car that keeps bouncing after a bump needs new shocks. A **strut** combines a spring and shock in one unit.
- **Control arms** and **ball joints** connect the wheels to the frame and let them move up and down and steer. A **sway bar** reduces body lean in turns.
- **Rack-and-pinion steering** (most cars): turning the steering wheel spins a small **pinion** gear that moves a toothed **rack** left or right, pushing the wheels through the **tie rods**. **Power steering** uses a hydraulic pump or an electric motor to reduce effort.
- **Wheel alignment** adjusts three angles: **camber** (inward or outward tilt of the wheel seen from the front), **caster** (forward or backward tilt of the steering axis seen from the side), and **toe** (whether the fronts of the wheels point in or out seen from above). Bad alignment causes pulling and uneven tire wear.
- **Unbalanced wheels** cause vibration at certain speeds.

::: example Reading a tire size: P215/65R15
- **P** = passenger car tire
- **215** = tire width in **millimeters**
- **65** = aspect ratio: the sidewall height is **65% of the width**
- **R** = **radial** construction
- **15** = fits a wheel (rim) **15 inches** in diameter
:::

- **Underinflated** tires wear on **both outer edges**; **overinflated** tires wear in the **center**. Rotating tires regularly evens out wear.

## Troubleshooting: Symptom → Most Likely Cause

This table is worth memorizing. Many Auto questions describe a symptom and ask for the cause.

| Symptom | Most likely cause |
|---|---|
| Engine **cranks** normally but **won't start** | No **fuel** or no **spark** (fuel pump, clogged filter, ignition problem) |
| **Clicking** when you turn the key, but the engine **won't crank** | **Weak battery**, corroded or loose battery cables, or a bad starter solenoid |
| Battery keeps going dead; **battery warning light** on while driving | Bad **alternator** or loose/broken **drive belt** |
| Engine **overheats** | Low coolant, thermostat stuck closed, bad water pump, broken belt, clogged radiator, failed fan |
| **Blue-gray** exhaust smoke | Engine is **burning oil** (worn piston rings or valve seals) |
| **Black** exhaust smoke | Too much fuel (**rich** mixture) — clogged air filter, leaking injector |
| Thick **white** smoke with a sweet smell | **Coolant** burning — often a blown **head gasket** |
| **Pinging or knocking** when accelerating | Fuel octane too low, ignition timing too early, or engine overheating |
| Engine **misfires** or idles roughly | Bad spark plug, plug wire, or coil; vacuum leak |
| **Squeal** when starting or turning the wheel | Loose or worn **drive belt** |
| Brakes **squeal** | Pads worn to the **wear indicator** |
| Brakes **grind** | Pads worn through — **metal on metal** |
| **Spongy** brake pedal | **Air in the brake lines** or low brake fluid |
| Brake pedal **pulsates** | Warped brake **rotors** |
| Car **pulls** to one side | Wheel **alignment**, uneven tire pressure, or a sticking brake |
| **Vibration** at highway speed | Unbalanced wheels or a bent wheel |
| **Clicking while turning** (front-wheel drive) | Worn **CV joint** |
| Car keeps **bouncing** after bumps | Worn **shock absorbers** |
| **Rotten-egg** smell from the exhaust | Catalytic converter problem |
| Engine races but the car barely speeds up (manual) | **Slipping clutch** |
{.compact}
