# Your Study Plan {data-sub="Find your starting point, pick a schedule, and study the way that actually works"}

The fastest way to a higher score is not studying *more* — it is studying the **right things** in the **right way**, a little every day. This chapter gives you a plan for eight weeks, four weeks, or two weeks, plus the study methods that make information stick.

## Step 1: Find Your Starting Point

Before you study anything else, take **Practice Test 1** in Part IV of this book under real conditions:

- Set a timer for each subtest using the official time limits.
- No calculator, no phone, no notes. Scratch paper only.
- Do it in one sitting if you can, the way the real test works.

Then score it and fill in the **Score Tracker** at the end of this chapter. Your results show you exactly where your points are hiding.

::: tip Your "AFQT core" percentage
Add up the questions you got right in **Arithmetic Reasoning, Word Knowledge, Paragraph Comprehension, and Mathematics Knowledge**, then divide by the number of questions in those four sections. That single number is the best way to track your progress toward your AFQT goal.
:::

## Step 2: Set Your Priorities

1. **The AFQT four come first.** Verbal (WK + PC) is half of your AFQT, and AR and MK are a quarter each. Until your AFQT core is consistently above 80%, spend about **70% of your study time** on these four.
2. **Attack your weakest AFQT area first.** Raising a weak area from 50% to 75% adds far more than raising a strong area from 85% to 90%.
3. **Then target your job.** Once your AFQT core is strong, look at the line score for the job you want (Chapter 2) and study the technical subtests that feed it.

## Study Methods That Actually Work

**1. Active recall.** Reading something over and over feels productive, but it does not build memory. Close the book and *say or write* the idea from memory, then check. Flashcards and practice questions are active recall.

**2. Spaced repetition.** Review material just as you are about to forget it. A simple way is the **three-box system**: new cards go in Box 1 (review daily). When you get a card right, move it to Box 2 (review every three days). Right again? Box 3 (review weekly). Miss a card at any time and it goes back to Box 1.

**3. The error log.** Every missed question goes into your log with the reason you missed it:

- **Didn't know it** → learn the fact or method, then make a flashcard.
- **Misread it** → write the word you missed (NOT, remaining, per...).
- **Careless mistake** → write the exact slip (dropped a negative sign, forgot to convert minutes).

Then redo the question two or three days later without looking at the explanation.

**4. Mixed practice.** Once you know several problem types, practice them **mixed together**. On the real test you must recognize the type before you can solve it.

**5. Timed practice.** Do at least one timed set every few days so the pace of the real test feels normal.

**6. Teach it.** Explain a concept out loud as if to a friend. If you get stuck, you have found exactly what to review.

**7. Sleep on it.** Your brain locks in what you studied while you sleep. Studying late into the night before the test does more harm than good.

## Building Vocabulary Fast

- Learn **15–20 new words a day** from the list in Chapter 5. Write the word on the front of a card and the meaning plus your own example sentence on the back.
- Learn **word parts** (prefixes, roots, suffixes). One root such as *bene* ("good") unlocks *benefit, benevolent, beneficial,* and *benefactor*.
- **Read 15 minutes a day** — news articles, science magazines, history, biographies. When you meet an unfamiliar word, guess its meaning from context, then look it up.
- **Use new words** when you speak and write. Using a word three times makes it yours.

## Building Math Skill Fast

- Do **every** problem by hand. No calculator, ever, while you study.
- Memorize the **multiplication facts to 12 × 12**, the **perfect squares to 15²**, and the common **fraction–decimal–percent** equivalents in Chapter 7.
- When you miss a problem, **rework it from a blank page** two days later.
- Learn to **recognize problem types** (percent change, distance–rate–time, work together). Recognition is half the battle.

## Chapter Summary

- Start with a **diagnostic** practice test and track your **AFQT core %**.
- Spend most of your time on **WK, PC, AR, and MK** until your AFQT core is above 80%.
- Study **a little every day** using active recall, spaced repetition, and an error log.
- Use timed, mixed practice so the real test feels familiar.
