# Test-Taking Strategy {data-sub="How to pick up every point you have earned — on the computer test and on paper"}

Two people with the same knowledge can walk out of the ASVAB with very different scores. The difference is strategy: reading carefully, pacing well, eliminating wrong answers, and staying calm. This chapter gives you the habits that turn what you know into points.

## The Seven Golden Rules

<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Read the whole question.</strong> The last sentence tells you what to find. "How much <em>more</em>," "how many are <em>left</em>," and "which is <em>NOT</em>" change everything.</li>
<li><strong>Predict before you peek.</strong> Try to answer in your head before you look at the choices. Wrong answers are designed to look attractive; a prediction protects you from them.</li>
<li><strong>Read every choice.</strong> Choice A may look good, but D may be better. Questions often ask for the <em>best</em> answer.</li>
<li><strong>Eliminate, then choose.</strong> Cross out the choices you know are wrong. Each one you remove raises your odds.</li>
<li><strong>Use your scratch paper.</strong> Write out every math step, sketch every diagram, and keep a list of letters you have eliminated.</li>
<li><strong>Check that your answer makes sense.</strong> Is it the right size? The right units? Did you answer the question that was actually asked?</li>
<li><strong>Keep moving.</strong> One hard question is worth the same as one easy question. Do not let it steal time or confidence from the rest.</li>
</ol>

## Strategies for the Computer Test (CAT-ASVAB and PiCAT)

**1. Make every answer final before you submit it.** You cannot skip or return to questions. Before you confirm, ask: "Did I answer what was asked? Did I read all four choices?"

**2. Use the time you are given.** The time limits on the proctored CAT are generous, and almost everyone finishes. Careful beats fast. There is no prize for finishing early.

**3. Know your pace.** Here is roughly how much time you have per question on the proctored CAT-ASVAB (when tryout questions are added, the time limit grows too, so the pace stays about the same).

| Subtest | Questions | Time | Time per question |
|---|---|---|---|
| General Science | 15 | 12 min | about 48 seconds |
| Arithmetic Reasoning | 15 | 55 min | about 3 min 40 sec |
| Word Knowledge | 15 | 9 min | about 36 seconds |
| Paragraph Comprehension | 10 | 27 min | about 2 min 40 sec |
| Mathematics Knowledge | 15 | 31 min | about 2 minutes |
| Electronics Information | 15 | 10 min | about 40 seconds |
| Auto Information | 10 | 7 min | about 42 seconds |
| Shop Information | 10 | 6 min | about 36 seconds |
| Mechanical Comprehension | 15 | 22 min | about 1½ minutes |
| Assembling Objects | 15 | 18 min | about 1 min 12 sec |
{.compact}

Notice that the **knowledge subtests are fast** (WK, GS, EI, AI, SI): you either know the word or fact or you do not, so do not linger. The **reasoning subtests are slow** (AR, PC, MK, MC): use that time to work carefully on paper.

**4. Hard questions are a good sign.** The test gives harder questions to people who are doing well. Stay calm and keep working.

**5. If time runs short, keep answering for real.** Unfinished questions are penalized as if you had guessed randomly, and a string of wrong answers at the end of a subtest costs a lot. If you see the clock running low, read each remaining question quickly and give your best genuine answer.

**6. Do not try to "game" the test.** Some people believe they should answer early questions extra slowly or miss easy questions on purpose. Do not. The scoring system is designed to prevent coachable tricks. Simply give each question your honest best effort.

**7. On the PiCAT, set up a real test environment.** There are no subtest time limits, but take it in one quiet sitting, with scratch paper and a pencil, and no calculator, phone, or help. Your Verification Test will need to match.

## Strategies for the Paper Test

**1. Use two passes.** Answer every question you can do quickly. Circle the hard ones in your test booklet and come back to them once you reach the end of the subtest.

**2. Never leave a blank.** A blank is always wrong, and there is no extra penalty for guessing. In the last minute of each subtest, fill in an answer for every question you have not done.

**3. Watch your bubbles.** When you skip a question, make sure the next answer goes in the right row. Every few questions, check that the number in your booklet matches the number on your answer sheet.

**4. Know your pace.** The paper test is faster than the computer test:

| Subtest | Questions | Time | Time per question |
|---|---|---|---|
| General Science | 25 | 11 min | about 26 seconds |
| Arithmetic Reasoning | 30 | 36 min | about 72 seconds |
| Word Knowledge | 35 | 11 min | about 19 seconds |
| Paragraph Comprehension | 15 | 13 min | about 52 seconds |
| Mathematics Knowledge | 25 | 24 min | about 58 seconds |
| Electronics Information | 20 | 9 min | about 27 seconds |
| Auto & Shop Information | 25 | 11 min | about 26 seconds |
| Mechanical Comprehension | 25 | 19 min | about 46 seconds |
| Assembling Objects | 25 | 15 min | about 36 seconds |
{.compact}

## Smart Guessing: The Process of Elimination

On almost every hard question, you know *something*. Use it to cross out wrong answers. If you can eliminate two of the four choices, a guess between the last two is right half the time instead of a quarter of the time.

::: example Example: eliminate with estimation
A jacket costs $79.95 and is on sale for 20% off. What is the sale price?

A) $15.99  B) $59.95  C) $63.96  D) $95.94

**Think:** The price is about $80, and 20% of $80 is $16, so the sale price is about $80 − $16 = $64.

- A ($15.99) is the *discount*, not the sale price — a classic trap.
- D is *more* than the original price — impossible for a sale.
- B is too low.
- **C ($63.96)** matches the estimate. Check: $79.95 × 0.80 = $63.96.
:::

::: example Example: work backward from the answers
If 3x + 7 = 31, what is x?

A) 6  B) 7  C) 8  D) 9

If you blank on the algebra, **test the choices**. Try C: 3(8) + 7 = 24 + 7 = 31. It works, so the answer is **C**. Testing the middle choices first is efficient, because if the result is too big or too small you know which way to go.
:::

### Elimination clues for each kind of question

| Subtest | Clues that a choice is wrong |
|---|---|
| **Word Knowledge** | Wrong "charge" (positive vs. negative meaning), wrong part of speech, or a word that only *sounds* like the test word |
| **Paragraph Comprehension** | Not stated or supported in the passage; too extreme ("always," "never," "all"); too broad or too narrow for a main-idea question |
| **Arithmetic Reasoning** | Wrong size (a sale price higher than the original), wrong units, or the answer to a middle step instead of the final step |
| **Mathematics Knowledge** | Fails when you plug it back in; impossible values (a negative length, a probability above 1) |
| **Technical subtests** | Breaks a physical rule you know (water flowing uphill by itself, a smaller gear turning slower than the larger gear it drives) |

## Read the Question, Not What You Expect

Many lost points have nothing to do with knowledge. They come from misreading. Underline (in your mind or on scratch paper) these words whenever you see them:

- **NOT, EXCEPT, LEAST** — you are looking for the one that does *not* fit.
- **More than / less than / difference** — subtract, and watch the order.
- **Remaining, left, still** — subtract what was used from the starting amount.
- **Each, per, every** — a rate; you may need to multiply or divide.
- **Total, altogether, combined** — add or multiply everything.
- **Units** — minutes or hours? Inches or feet? Dollars or cents? Convert *before* you calculate.

## Mental Math Survival Kit

There is no calculator on the ASVAB, so these quick tricks save time and prevent errors. (Chapter 7 covers them in full.)

| Trick | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Multiply by 5 | Multiply by 10, then halve | 46 × 5 = 460 ÷ 2 = 230 |
| Multiply by 25 | Multiply by 100, then divide by 4 | 36 × 25 = 3,600 ÷ 4 = 900 |
| Multiply by 9 | Multiply by 10, then subtract the number | 9 × 47 = 470 − 47 = 423 |
| Multiply by 11 | Two-digit number: put the sum of the digits in the middle | 11 × 43 = 4 (4+3) 3 = 473 |
| 10% of anything | Move the decimal one place left | 10% of 365 = 36.5 |
| 5% | Half of 10% | 5% of 365 = 18.25 |
| 15% (tips) | 10% plus half of that | 15% of $40 = $4 + $2 = $6 |
| Break it apart | Split a number into easy pieces | 7 × 68 = 7 × 60 + 7 × 8 = 420 + 56 = 476 |

## Your Body Is Part of the Test

- **Sleep** seven to nine hours for the two nights before the test. One good night is not enough after a week of short ones.
- **Eat** a normal breakfast with some protein. Avoid a sugar crash.
- **Drink** water, but not so much that you will be distracted.
- **Use the restroom** right before testing begins.
- **Dress in layers.** Testing rooms can be cold or warm.

::: tip If your mind goes blank
Stop for ten seconds. Breathe in slowly for four counts, hold for four, breathe out for four, and hold for four. Then reread the question one phrase at a time and write down anything you know about it. Starting to write almost always restarts your thinking.
:::

## Chapter Summary

- **Read carefully, predict, eliminate,** and check that answers make sense.
- **Computer test:** no skipping or going back; time is generous; never leave questions unfinished — and never random-click at the end.
- **Paper test:** two passes; never leave a blank; check your bubbles.
- Use **estimation** and **back-solving** when you are stuck.
- Treat sleep, food, and calm breathing as part of your preparation.
