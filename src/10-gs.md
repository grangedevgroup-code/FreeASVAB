# General Science {data-sub="Life science, earth and space science, chemistry, and physics — the facts that show up most"}

General Science (GS) covers the physical and biological sciences you met in middle and high school. On the computer test you get **15 questions in 12 minutes** — under a minute each — so GS is mostly about **knowing facts**, not long reasoning.

GS is not part of the AFQT, but it feeds many line scores: Army **EL, GM, and ST**; Air Force **Electronics**; and Navy and Coast Guard technical and medical ratings. If you want an electronics, medical, or technical job, GS matters.

::: strategy How to study General Science
Read one section a day, then quiz yourself on its Key Idea boxes and tables. Science facts stick best as **flashcards** and **pictures**. Do not try to memorize everything at once — focus on the bolded terms; they are the ones the test asks about.
:::

## Science Basics

### The scientific method

1. **Observe** something and ask a **question**.
2. Form a **hypothesis** — a testable, educated guess.
3. Run an **experiment**. Change only one thing on purpose (the **independent variable**) and measure what happens (the **dependent variable**). Keep everything else the same (**constants**), and compare with a **control group** that gets no treatment.
4. **Analyze** the data and draw a **conclusion**.

A **theory** is a well-tested explanation of *why* something happens (the theory of evolution, atomic theory). A **law** describes *what* happens, often with an equation (Newton's law of gravity). Theories do not "graduate" into laws — they are different things.

## Cells

The **cell theory** says that all living things are made of cells, the cell is the basic unit of life, and all cells come from other cells.

- **Prokaryotic** cells have **no nucleus** (bacteria).
- **Eukaryotic** cells **have a nucleus** (plants, animals, fungi, and protists).

| Cell part | Job | Found in |
|---|---|---|
| **Nucleus** | Control center; holds the DNA | Plant and animal cells |
| **Cell membrane** | Controls what enters and leaves | All cells |
| **Cytoplasm** | Jelly-like fluid where the organelles sit | All cells |
| **Mitochondria** | "Powerhouse": release energy from food (cellular respiration) | Plant and animal cells |
| **Ribosomes** | Build proteins | All cells |
| **Endoplasmic reticulum** | Transports materials; rough ER has ribosomes | Plant and animal cells |
| **Golgi apparatus** | Packages and ships proteins | Plant and animal cells |
| **Lysosomes** | Break down wastes and worn-out parts | Mainly animal cells |
| **Vacuole** | Storage; plants have one large central vacuole | Plant (large) and animal (small) |
| **Chloroplasts** | Photosynthesis (contain green chlorophyll) | **Plant cells only** |
| **Cell wall** | Rigid outer support (made of cellulose in plants) | **Plants**, fungi, and bacteria — **not animals** |
{.compact}

::: key Plant cells vs. animal cells
Plant cells have three things animal cells do not: a **cell wall**, **chloroplasts**, and a **large central vacuole**.
:::

### How things move in and out of cells

- **Diffusion:** particles spread from an area of **high concentration to low concentration**, with no energy needed.
- **Osmosis:** the diffusion of **water** across a membrane.
- **Active transport:** the cell **uses energy** to move particles from low to high concentration.

### Cell division

| | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Growth and repair | Making sex cells (eggs and sperm) |
| Number of cells produced | 2 | 4 |
| Chromosomes in each new cell | Same as the parent (46 in humans) | Half the parent (23 in humans) |
| Are the new cells identical? | Yes | No |
{.compact}

## Genetics

- **DNA** (deoxyribonucleic acid) carries genetic instructions. It is shaped like a twisted ladder called a **double helix**. Its bases pair **A with T** and **C with G**. (RNA uses **U** instead of T.)
- A **gene** is a section of DNA that codes for a trait. Genes are carried on **chromosomes**. Humans have **46 chromosomes (23 pairs)**.
- Different versions of a gene are called **alleles**. A **dominant** allele (capital letter, T) shows up whenever it is present. A **recessive** allele (lowercase, t) shows up only when there are two copies (tt).
- **Genotype** = the gene combination (Tt). **Phenotype** = the trait you can see (tall).
- **Homozygous** = two identical alleles (TT or tt). **Heterozygous** = two different alleles (Tt).
- **Gregor Mendel**, who experimented with pea plants, is called the father of genetics.

::: example A Punnett square
Two pea plants, both **Tt** (T = tall, dominant; t = short), are crossed.

| | **T** | **t** |
|---|---|---|
| **T** | TT | Tt |
| **t** | Tt | tt |

Results: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt. Three of four offspring (75%) are **tall**; one of four (**25%**) is **short**.
:::

**Sex chromosomes:** females are **XX** and males are **XY**. The father's sperm carries either an X or a Y, so the father's chromosome determines the baby's sex.

## Classifying Living Things

Scientists use **binomial nomenclature**, a two-part Latin name: *Genus species* (humans are *Homo sapiens*). This system comes from **Carolus Linnaeus**.

::: memory The classification levels, from biggest to smallest
**D**ear **K**ing **P**hilip **C**ame **O**ver **F**or **G**ood **S**oup:
**D**omain, **K**ingdom, **P**hylum, **C**lass, **O**rder, **F**amily, **G**enus, **S**pecies.
:::

- The three **domains** are **Bacteria, Archaea,** and **Eukarya**.
- Six **kingdoms** are commonly taught: **animals, plants, fungi** (mushrooms, yeast, molds — they absorb food and do not photosynthesize), **protists** (mostly single-celled, like amoebas and algae), **bacteria**, and **archaea**.
- **Vertebrates** (animals with backbones): fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, and mammals. Birds and mammals are **warm-blooded**; fish, amphibians, and reptiles are **cold-blooded**.
- **Invertebrates** have no backbone: insects (**6 legs**, 3 body parts), arachnids such as spiders (**8 legs**), crustaceans, mollusks, worms, and jellyfish.
- **Viruses** are not cells. They can reproduce only inside a living host cell.

## Plants

::: formula Photosynthesis and cellular respiration
**Photosynthesis** (in chloroplasts):
carbon dioxide + water + light energy → glucose (sugar) + oxygen
6CO~{2} + 6H~{2}O → C~{6}H~{12}O~{6} + 6O~{2}

**Cellular respiration** (in mitochondria, in plants AND animals):
glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy
:::

- **Roots** absorb water and minerals and anchor the plant. **Stems** support and transport. **Leaves** carry out photosynthesis. **Flowers** are for reproduction.
- **Xylem** carries water **up** from the roots; **phloem** carries food (sugars) throughout the plant.
- Tiny pores called **stomata** on leaves let gases in and out. Water evaporating from leaves is called **transpiration**.
- **Pollination** moves pollen to the female part of a flower, leading to seeds. A seed sprouting is **germination**.

## The Human Body

| System | Main parts | Main job |
|---|---|---|
| **Skeletal** | 206 bones (adult), cartilage | Support, protection, movement; bone marrow makes blood cells; stores calcium |
| **Muscular** | Skeletal (voluntary), smooth (involuntary, in organs), and cardiac (heart) muscle | Movement. **Tendons** connect muscle to bone; **ligaments** connect bone to bone |
| **Circulatory** | Heart, arteries, veins, capillaries, blood | Carries oxygen, nutrients, and wastes |
| **Respiratory** | Nose, trachea, bronchi, lungs, alveoli, diaphragm | Takes in oxygen; removes carbon dioxide |
| **Digestive** | Mouth, esophagus, stomach, small and large intestines, liver, gallbladder, pancreas | Breaks down food and absorbs nutrients |
| **Nervous** | Brain, spinal cord, nerves (neurons) | Control and communication |
| **Endocrine** | Glands: pituitary, thyroid, adrenal, pancreas, ovaries, testes | Chemical messages (hormones) |
| **Immune / lymphatic** | White blood cells, lymph nodes, spleen | Fights disease |
| **Excretory / urinary** | Kidneys, bladder | Filters blood and removes liquid waste (urine) |
| **Integumentary** | Skin, hair, nails | Protection; skin is the **largest organ** |
{.compact}

**Heart and blood**

- The heart has **four chambers**: two upper **atria** (receive blood) and two lower **ventricles** (pump blood out).
- **Arteries** carry blood **away** from the heart (**A** for **A**way). **Veins** carry blood back to the heart. **Capillaries** are tiny vessels where oxygen and nutrients pass into the cells.
- **Red blood cells** carry oxygen using hemoglobin. **White blood cells** fight infection. **Platelets** help blood clot. **Plasma** is the liquid part of blood.

**Digestion step by step:** chewing and saliva in the **mouth** → **esophagus** → **stomach** (acid and enzymes) → **small intestine** (most digestion and absorption of nutrients) → **large intestine** (absorbs water) → waste leaves the body. The **liver** makes bile, the **gallbladder** stores it, and the **pancreas** makes digestive enzymes and the hormone **insulin**.

**The brain:** the **cerebrum** handles thinking, memory, and the senses; the **cerebellum** controls balance and coordination; the **brain stem (medulla)** controls automatic functions such as breathing and heart rate.

**Hormones:** the **pituitary** is the "master gland"; the **thyroid** controls metabolism; the **adrenal glands** make adrenaline; **insulin** from the pancreas **lowers** blood sugar.

**Bones to know:** the **femur** (thigh bone) is the longest bone; the **stapes** (in the ear) is the smallest. The skull protects the brain, and the rib cage protects the heart and lungs.

**Germs and disease**

- **Bacteria** cause strep throat, tuberculosis, and tetanus. They can be treated with **antibiotics**. (Alexander **Fleming** discovered penicillin, the first antibiotic, in 1928.)
- **Viruses** cause colds, influenza, measles, and COVID-19. **Antibiotics do not work on viruses.** **Vaccines** help prevent viral diseases. (Edward **Jenner** developed the first vaccine, for smallpox.)
- **Fungi** cause athlete's foot and ringworm. A **protist** causes malaria, which mosquitoes spread.
- Louis **Pasteur** proved that germs cause disease and developed **pasteurization** (heating liquids to kill germs).

## Ecology

- An **ecosystem** is all the living things in an area plus their nonliving surroundings. A **population** is one species in an area; a **community** is all the populations together. A **habitat** is where an organism lives; its **niche** is its role.
- **Producers** (plants, algae) make their own food. **Consumers** eat other organisms: **herbivores** eat plants, **carnivores** eat animals, **omnivores** eat both. **Decomposers** (bacteria and fungi) break down dead material and return nutrients to the soil.
- A **food chain** shows one path of energy (grass → rabbit → hawk). A **food web** shows many connected chains.
- Only about **10%** of the energy at one level of a food chain passes to the next. That is why there are far fewer hawks than rabbits.

| Relationship | Who benefits | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Mutualism** | Both benefit (+/+) | Bees and flowers |
| **Commensalism** | One benefits; the other is unaffected (+/0) | Barnacles riding on a whale |
| **Parasitism** | One benefits; the other is harmed (+/−) | Ticks on a dog |
| **Predation** | Predator eats prey | Wolf and deer |
{.compact}

**Biomes:** tundra (cold, treeless, permafrost), taiga (cold coniferous forest), temperate deciduous forest (trees lose leaves), tropical rainforest (hot, wet, most species), grassland (prairie, savanna), desert (very dry), and aquatic (fresh water and oceans).

## Earth's Layers

{{fig:gs-earth-layers|Earth's layers, from the thin crust to the solid inner core}}

- **Crust:** the thin, rocky outer layer. Oceanic crust is thinner and denser than continental crust.
- **Mantle:** the thickest layer; hot rock that flows very slowly.
- **Outer core:** **liquid** iron and nickel. Its motion creates Earth's **magnetic field**.
- **Inner core:** **solid** iron and nickel; the hottest layer. It stays solid because of enormous pressure.

The crust and the top of the mantle form rigid **plates** (the lithosphere) that move a few centimeters a year.

## Plate Tectonics, Earthquakes, and Volcanoes

| Plate boundary | Plates move... | Results |
|---|---|---|
| **Divergent** | Apart | Mid-ocean ridges, rift valleys, new crust |
| **Convergent** | Together | Mountains, deep ocean trenches, volcanoes (one plate sinks under the other in **subduction**) |
| **Transform** | Past each other | Earthquakes (California's San Andreas Fault) |
{.compact}

- An earthquake starts underground at the **focus**; the spot on the surface directly above it is the **epicenter**. Earthquakes are recorded with a **seismograph**. On magnitude scales, each whole number is **10 times** more ground shaking (and about 32 times more energy).
- Molten rock underground is **magma**; once it reaches the surface it is **lava**.
- Long ago the continents were joined in one supercontinent, **Pangaea** (Alfred Wegener's idea of continental drift).

## Rocks and Minerals

| Rock type | How it forms | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Igneous** | Magma or lava cools and hardens | Granite, basalt, obsidian, pumice |
| **Sedimentary** | Layers of sediment are pressed and cemented together; may contain **fossils** | Sandstone, limestone, shale, conglomerate |
| **Metamorphic** | Existing rock is changed by heat and pressure | Marble (from limestone), slate (from shale), quartzite (from sandstone), gneiss |
{.compact}

::: memory Rock types
**Igneous** = **ignite** (fire, melted rock). **Sedimentary** = **sediment** (layers). **Metamorphic** = **metamorphosis** (changed).
:::

- A **mineral** is a naturally occurring, inorganic solid with a crystal structure.
- **Mohs hardness scale** (1 = softest, 10 = hardest): 1 talc, 2 gypsum, 3 calcite, 4 fluorite, 5 apatite, 6 feldspar, 7 quartz, 8 topaz, 9 corundum, **10 diamond**. A harder mineral scratches a softer one.
- **Weathering** breaks rock down (ice wedging, roots, acid rain). **Erosion** carries the pieces away (water, wind, glaciers, gravity). **Deposition** drops them in a new place.
- In undisturbed rock layers, the **oldest layers are at the bottom** (the law of superposition).
- **Geologic eras:** Precambrian (the oldest and longest), Paleozoic, Mesozoic (the age of dinosaurs), and Cenozoic (the age of mammals; today).

## The Atmosphere and Weather

**Air** is about **78% nitrogen**, **21% oxygen**, about 1% argon, and small amounts of carbon dioxide and water vapor.

{{fig:gs-atmosphere|The layers of the atmosphere}}

::: memory Layers from the ground up
**T**he **S**trong **M**an's **T**riceps **E**xplode: **T**roposphere, **S**tratosphere, **M**esosphere, **T**hermosphere, **E**xosphere.
:::

- **Troposphere:** where we live and where almost all **weather** happens; it gets colder as you go up.
- **Stratosphere:** contains the **ozone layer**, which absorbs harmful ultraviolet (UV) light.
- **Mesosphere:** where most **meteors burn up**; the coldest layer.
- **Thermosphere:** very thin, very hot air; **auroras** happen here, and the International Space Station orbits here.
- **Exosphere:** the outermost layer, fading into space.

| Instrument | Measures |
|---|---|
| Thermometer | Temperature |
| **Barometer** | **Air pressure** |
| Anemometer | Wind speed |
| Wind vane | Wind direction |
| Hygrometer | Humidity |
| Rain gauge | Rainfall |
{.compact}

**Weather patterns**

- **High pressure** usually brings clear, fair weather. **Low pressure** usually brings clouds and storms.
- Wind blows from **high pressure toward low pressure**.
- A **cold front** (cold air pushing under warm air) brings sudden storms and a quick temperature drop. A **warm front** brings steady, light rain and slowly rising temperatures.
- **Clouds:** **cirrus** (high, thin, wispy, made of ice), **cumulus** (puffy, fair weather), **stratus** (low, gray layers). *Nimbus* means rain: **cumulonimbus** clouds bring thunderstorms.
- **Hurricanes** form over **warm ocean water** and have a calm **eye** at the center. **Tornadoes** are narrow, violently spinning columns of air that reach the ground.
- **Sea breeze:** during the day, cooler air blows from the sea toward the warmer land. At night, a **land breeze** blows the other way.
- **Weather** is day-to-day conditions; **climate** is the average over many years.
- The **greenhouse effect**: gases such as carbon dioxide trap heat near Earth's surface. Without it, Earth would be much colder; adding more of these gases warms the planet.

## Water and Oceans

- About **71%** of Earth's surface is water. About **97%** of Earth's water is salty ocean water; most fresh water is frozen in ice caps and glaciers.
- **The water cycle:** **evaporation** (liquid to vapor) and **transpiration** (from plants) → **condensation** (vapor forms clouds) → **precipitation** (rain, snow) → **runoff** and **infiltration** into the ground (groundwater, aquifers).
- **Tides** are caused mainly by the **Moon's gravity**, with help from the Sun. Most coasts get two high tides and two low tides about every day. The biggest tides (**spring tides**) come at new moon and full moon, when the Sun, Moon, and Earth line up. The smallest (**neap tides**) come at the quarter moons.

## Astronomy

### The solar system

{{fig:gs-planets|The planets in order from the Sun (sizes not to scale with distances)}}

::: memory The planets in order
**M**y **V**ery **E**ducated **M**other **J**ust **S**erved **U**s **N**achos:
**M**ercury, **V**enus, **E**arth, **M**ars, **J**upiter, **S**aturn, **U**ranus, **N**eptune.
:::

- The **Sun** is a star — a ball of hot gas powered by **nuclear fusion** (hydrogen fusing into helium). It holds about 99.8% of the solar system's mass. Its light takes about **8 minutes** to reach Earth.
- The **inner planets** (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) are small and rocky. The **outer planets** are giants made mostly of gas and ice. The **asteroid belt** lies between Mars and Jupiter.
- **Mercury** is closest to the Sun and smallest. **Venus** is the **hottest** planet (its thick atmosphere traps heat) and the brightest planet in our sky. **Mars** is red because of iron oxide (rust) and has two small moons. **Jupiter** is the **largest** planet, with the Great Red Spot storm. **Saturn** has the most visible rings. **Uranus** spins tipped on its side. **Neptune** is the farthest planet.
- **Pluto** was reclassified as a **dwarf planet** in 2006.

### Earth and the Moon

- Earth **rotates** (spins) once about every **24 hours**, which causes day and night. It spins west to east, so the Sun rises in the **east**.
- Earth **revolves** (orbits) the Sun once about every **365¼ days** — one year. The extra quarter day adds up to a **leap year** every four years.
- **Seasons are caused by the tilt of Earth's axis** (about 23.5°), **not** by Earth's distance from the Sun. When the Northern Hemisphere tilts toward the Sun, it has summer. The **summer solstice** (around June 21) is the longest day in the Northern Hemisphere; the **winter solstice** (around December 21) is the shortest. On the **equinoxes** (around March 20 and September 22), day and night are about equal.
- The Moon shines by **reflected sunlight**. The same side always faces Earth. It goes through its **phases** in about **29.5 days**: new moon → waxing crescent → first quarter → waxing gibbous → **full moon** → waning gibbous → third quarter → waning crescent. **Waxing** means growing; **waning** means shrinking.

{{fig:gs-eclipse|Solar and lunar eclipses}}

- **Solar eclipse:** the **Moon passes between the Sun and Earth**, blocking the Sun. It can happen only at **new moon**.
- **Lunar eclipse:** **Earth passes between the Sun and the Moon**, and Earth's shadow falls on the Moon. It can happen only at **full moon**.

### Stars and beyond

- A star's color shows its temperature: **blue stars are hottest**, red stars are coolest. Our Sun is a medium-sized yellow star.
- A **light-year** is a **distance** — how far light travels in a year (about 6 trillion miles) — not a measure of time.
- The nearest star after the Sun is **Proxima Centauri**, about 4.2 light-years away.
- We live in the **Milky Way**, a spiral **galaxy**.
- A **meteoroid** is a space rock; a **meteor** is its streak of light in our atmosphere; a **meteorite** is a piece that reaches the ground. A **comet** is a ball of ice and dust whose tail points **away from the Sun**.

## Matter and Its States

**Matter** is anything that has **mass** and takes up space (**volume**).

| State | Shape | Volume | Particles |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Solid** | Definite | Definite | Packed tightly; vibrate in place |
| **Liquid** | Takes the shape of its container | Definite | Close together but slide past each other |
| **Gas** | Fills its container | Fills its container | Far apart and fast-moving |
| **Plasma** | No definite shape | No definite volume | Electrically charged gas (stars, lightning) |
{.compact}

| Change | From → to | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Melting | solid → liquid | Ice melting |
| Freezing | liquid → solid | Water freezing |
| Evaporation / boiling | liquid → gas | A puddle drying |
| Condensation | gas → liquid | Water drops on a cold glass |
| **Sublimation** | **solid → gas** | **Dry ice** (frozen carbon dioxide) |
| Deposition | gas → solid | Frost forming |
{.compact}

**Physical vs. chemical changes**

- A **physical change** changes form or state but **not** what the substance is: melting, freezing, cutting, crushing, dissolving sugar in water.
- A **chemical change** makes a **new substance**: burning, **rusting**, cooking, digesting, souring milk. Signs include gas bubbles, a color change, heat or light given off, or a solid forming in a liquid.

**Density = mass ÷ volume.** Water's density is **1 gram per cubic centimeter** (1 g/mL). Things **less dense than water float**; things more dense sink. Ice floats because it is less dense than liquid water.

**Mass vs. weight:** **mass** is the amount of matter (kilograms) and is the same everywhere. **Weight** is the pull of gravity on that mass. On the Moon, you would weigh about **one-sixth** as much, but your mass would not change.

## Atoms and the Periodic Table

{{fig:gs-atom|A simple model of an atom}}

| Particle | Charge | Location |
|---|---|---|
| **Proton** | Positive (+) | Nucleus |
| **Neutron** | No charge (neutral) | Nucleus |
| **Electron** | Negative (−) | Moving around the nucleus in energy levels (shells) |
{.compact}

- The **atomic number** is the number of **protons**. It tells you which element the atom is.
- The **mass number** = protons + neutrons.
- In a neutral atom, the number of **electrons equals the number of protons**.
- **Isotopes** are atoms of the same element with different numbers of **neutrons** (carbon-12 and carbon-14).
- **Ions** are atoms that have gained electrons (negative ions) or lost electrons (positive ions).

**The periodic table** lists elements in order of atomic number. **Rows** are called **periods**; **columns** are **groups** or families, and elements in the same group behave alike.

- **Metals** (left side and middle) are shiny, conduct heat and electricity, and can be hammered into shapes. **Nonmetals** are on the right. **Metalloids** such as silicon are in between.
- **Group 1, alkali metals** (sodium, potassium): extremely reactive. **Group 17, halogens** (fluorine, chlorine): very reactive nonmetals. **Group 18, noble gases** (helium, neon, argon): almost never react.
- **Hydrogen** is the lightest and most abundant element in the universe. **Oxygen** is the most abundant element in Earth's crust. **Nitrogen** is the most abundant gas in the air.

## Elements, Compounds, and Mixtures

- An **element** contains only one kind of atom (gold, oxygen).
- A **compound** is two or more elements **chemically combined** in a fixed ratio (water, H~{2}O; table salt, NaCl). It can only be separated by a chemical reaction.
- A **mixture** is physically combined and can be separated by physical means (salad, sand and water, air). A **solution** is an evenly mixed mixture, such as salt water. The substance that dissolves is the **solute**; the substance it dissolves in is the **solvent**. Water is called the "universal solvent."
- In an **ionic bond**, electrons are **transferred** (usually metal + nonmetal, as in NaCl). In a **covalent bond**, electrons are **shared** (nonmetals, as in H~{2}O).

| Formula | Name | Formula | Name |
|---|---|---|---|
| H~{2}O | Water | NaCl | Table salt (sodium chloride) |
| CO~{2} | Carbon dioxide | CO | Carbon monoxide (poisonous) |
| O~{2} | Oxygen gas | O~{3} | Ozone |
| CH~{4} | Methane (natural gas) | NH~{3} | Ammonia |
| C~{6}H~{12}O~{6} | Glucose (sugar) | NaHCO~{3} | Baking soda |
| HCl | Hydrochloric acid (stomach acid) | H~{2}SO~{4} | Sulfuric acid (car batteries) |
{.compact}

## Chemical Reactions

- **Reactants → products.** Matter is not created or destroyed in a reaction (the **law of conservation of mass**), so equations must be **balanced**: 2H~{2} + O~{2} → 2H~{2}O.
- **Combustion** (burning): fuel + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy.
- **Exothermic** reactions **release** heat (burning wood). **Endothermic** reactions **absorb** heat (an instant cold pack).
- A **catalyst** speeds up a reaction without being used up. **Enzymes** are the catalysts in living things.
- **Rust** forms when iron reacts with oxygen and water (oxidation).

## Acids, Bases, and pH

{{fig:gs-ph|The pH scale}}

- The **pH scale** runs from **0 to 14**. **Below 7 is acidic**, **7 is neutral** (pure water), and **above 7 is basic** (alkaline). Each step is a **tenfold** change.
- **Acids** taste sour and turn blue litmus paper **red**: stomach acid, lemon juice, vinegar.
- **Bases** taste bitter, feel slippery, and turn red litmus paper **blue**: baking soda, ammonia, bleach, lye.
- An acid and a base **neutralize** each other, forming a salt and water.

## Motion and Forces

- **Speed** = distance ÷ time. **Velocity** is speed in a particular **direction**. **Acceleration** is the rate at which velocity changes.
- Near Earth's surface, gravity accelerates falling objects at about **9.8 m/s^2** (32 ft/s^2). Without air resistance, **all objects fall at the same rate**, no matter how heavy.

::: key Newton's three laws of motion
1. **Inertia:** an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at the same speed and direction, unless an unbalanced force acts on it. (This is why you need a seat belt.)
2. **F = m × a:** force equals mass times acceleration. The same push moves a light object faster than a heavy one.
3. **Action and reaction:** for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. (A rifle recoils; a rocket's exhaust pushes back and the rocket goes forward.)
:::

- **Momentum** = mass × velocity.
- **Friction** is a force that opposes motion between surfaces.
- **Gravity** pulls every mass toward every other mass. It is stronger for larger masses and weaker at greater distances.

## Energy

- **Kinetic energy** is energy of **motion** (a moving truck). **Potential energy** is **stored** energy (a rock on a cliff, a stretched spring, gasoline).
- **The law of conservation of energy:** energy cannot be created or destroyed — it only changes form.

| Device | Energy change |
|---|---|
| Battery | chemical → electrical |
| Electric motor | electrical → mechanical (motion) |
| Generator | mechanical → electrical |
| Light bulb | electrical → light (and heat) |
| Solar cell | light → electrical |
| Car engine | chemical → heat → mechanical |
| Photosynthesis | light → chemical |
{.compact}

- **Renewable** sources: solar, wind, hydroelectric (water), geothermal, biomass. **Nonrenewable**: coal, oil, natural gas, and uranium.
- **Nuclear fission** splits heavy atoms (nuclear power plants). **Nuclear fusion** joins light atoms (the Sun).

## Heat

- **Temperature** measures how fast the particles in a substance are moving (their average kinetic energy). **Heat** is energy that flows from a **hotter** object to a **colder** one.

| Heat transfer | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Conduction** | Direct contact | A metal spoon heating up in hot soup |
| **Convection** | Currents in a liquid or gas (hot fluid rises, cool fluid sinks) | Boiling water; a room heater warming the air |
| **Radiation** | Electromagnetic waves; needs no material | Sunlight; warmth from a campfire |
{.compact}

- **Conductors** (metals such as copper and aluminum) transfer heat easily. **Insulators** (wood, plastic, foam, air) slow it down.
- Most materials **expand when heated** and contract when cooled — that is why bridges have expansion joints.
- **Water** has a high specific heat: it takes a lot of energy to warm up and cools slowly. That is why coastal areas have milder climates.
- Water boils at a **lower** temperature at high altitude, where air pressure is lower.

## Waves, Sound, and Light

{{fig:gs-wave|Parts of a wave}}

- Waves carry **energy**. **Wavelength** is the distance from crest to crest. **Amplitude** is the height of the wave (bigger amplitude = louder sound or brighter light). **Frequency** is the number of waves per second, measured in **hertz (Hz)**.
- In **transverse waves** (light, ripples on a rope), the vibration is at right angles to the direction the wave travels. In **longitudinal** (compression) waves, such as **sound**, the vibration is along the direction of travel.

**Sound**

- Sound needs a **medium** (solid, liquid, or gas). It **cannot travel through empty space**.
- Sound travels **fastest in solids**, slower in liquids, and slowest in gases. In air it travels about **343 meters per second** (about 1,125 feet per second).
- **Pitch** depends on **frequency**: high frequency = high pitch. **Loudness** depends on **amplitude** and is measured in **decibels (dB)**.
- An **echo** is reflected sound. The **Doppler effect** is the change in pitch as a sound source moves toward you (higher) or away (lower), like a passing siren.
- Humans hear from about **20 to 20,000 Hz**. **Ultrasound** is above that range.

**Light and the electromagnetic spectrum**

- Light can travel through empty space at about **300,000 kilometers per second** (186,000 miles per second) — the fastest speed there is.

{{fig:gs-em|The electromagnetic spectrum}}

::: memory The electromagnetic spectrum, from long waves to short waves
**R**aging **M**artians **I**nvaded **V**enus **U**sing **X**-ray **G**uns: **R**adio, **M**icrowaves, **I**nfrared, **V**isible light, **U**ltraviolet, **X**-rays, **G**amma rays.
Moving right, wavelength gets **shorter** and frequency and **energy** get **higher**. Visible colors run **ROY G BIV** — **red** has the longest wavelength; **violet** has the shortest.
:::

- **Reflection:** light bounces off a surface. The angle it comes in equals the angle it leaves.
- **Refraction:** light **bends** when it passes from one material to another — the reason a straw looks bent in a glass of water. A **prism** separates white light into colors, as raindrops do in a rainbow.
- **Lenses:** a **convex** lens (thicker in the middle) brings light together; it is used in magnifying glasses and to correct **farsightedness**. A **concave** lens (thinner in the middle) spreads light out and corrects **nearsightedness**.
- An object looks red because it **reflects** red light and absorbs the other colors. **White** objects reflect all colors; **black** objects absorb them all (which is why black clothing gets hot in the sun).
- **Transparent** materials let light pass through clearly (glass); **translucent** materials let some light through (wax paper); **opaque** materials block light (wood).

## Magnetism (Electricity Is in Chapter 11)

- Every magnet has a **north pole** and a **south pole**. **Like poles repel; opposite poles attract.**
- Earth acts like a giant magnet, which is why a compass needle points north.
- Running electric current through a coil of wire wrapped around an iron core creates an **electromagnet**, which can be switched on and off.
