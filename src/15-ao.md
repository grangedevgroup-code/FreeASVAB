# Assembling Objects {data-sub="Spatial reasoning: where connections go and how pieces fit together"}

Assembling Objects (AO) measures **spatial ability** — how well you can picture how parts fit together. Every question is a picture. On the computer test you get **15 questions in 18 minutes** (about 1 minute 12 seconds each); the paper test has 25 questions in 15 minutes.

AO is **not** part of the AFQT, and it is not given on the student (high school) ASVAB. It is used mainly in some **Navy** job composites. Still, it is worth a little practice: the questions follow a few simple patterns, and careful checking turns them into easy points.

## The Two Kinds of AO Questions

### 1. Connection questions

The question box shows two shapes. Each shape has a point marked with a letter — **A** on one shape and **B** on the other. Your job is to find the answer choice in which a line connects **exactly those two points**.

{{fig:ao-example-connect|Example: point A is at a corner of the triangle; point B is at the center of the circle. Choice C is correct.}}

::: strategy How to solve connection questions
1. **Name each point in words.** "A is at a **corner** of the triangle. B is at the **center** of the circle." Saying it in words makes it stick.
2. **Check one shape at a time.** Go through the choices and cross out every one where the line does not touch the triangle at a corner. Then check the circle's end of the line.
3. **Expect the shapes to be turned.** The shapes in the answers may be rotated, and they may sit in different places. What matters is *where on each shape* the line attaches.
4. **Look closely at near misses.** The middle of a side is not a corner; a point on the edge of a circle is not the center; an inner corner of a star is not the tip of a point.
:::

### 2. Puzzle (assembly) questions

The question box shows several separate pieces. You must find the answer choice that shows **how the pieces fit together** into one figure.

{{fig:ao-example-puzzle|Example: a square cut into two pieces. Only choice B uses one triangle and one four-sided piece of exactly the right sizes.}}

::: strategy How to solve puzzle questions
1. **Count the pieces.** The correct answer uses **exactly** the same number of pieces. Cross out any choice with more or fewer.
2. **Find the most unusual piece** — the biggest one, or the one with a strange angle — and look for it in each choice.
3. **Check sizes and angles.** A long edge must match a long edge. A square corner stays a square corner. A small sliver cannot turn into a big wedge.
4. **Pieces may be turned, but not flipped.** In the answers, the pieces can be rotated in any direction. A mirror-image (flipped) version of a piece counts as a different piece.
5. **Eliminate, then confirm.** When you are down to one choice, quickly match every piece to be sure.
:::

::: tip Use your scratch paper
Sketch a rough copy of a tricky piece and mark its long side or its square corner. Comparing a quick sketch to each answer is often faster than flipping your eyes back and forth.
:::
