# How to Use This Book {.nonum data-sub="Read this first. It takes five minutes and will save you hours."}

This book has one job: to help you walk into the ASVAB ready, calm, and able to earn the highest score you are capable of. Whether you need to clear the enlistment minimum or want a top score that opens more jobs and incentives, this book is built to get you there and push you higher if you want to go further.

Everything is written in plain English. You do not need to remember anything from school to start. Each chapter begins at the basics, shows you worked examples step by step, and then gives you practice questions with full explanations.

## The Big Picture in 60 Seconds

- The **ASVAB** is the entrance test every branch of the U.S. military uses for enlisted applicants.
- It has **10 short subtests**: General Science, Arithmetic Reasoning, Word Knowledge, Paragraph Comprehension, Mathematics Knowledge, Electronics Information, Auto Information, Shop Information, Mechanical Comprehension, and Assembling Objects.
- Four of those subtests — **Word Knowledge, Paragraph Comprehension, Arithmetic Reasoning, and Mathematics Knowledge** — make up your **AFQT score**. The AFQT decides whether you can enlist at all.
- The other subtests, combined with the first four, make up your **line scores** (also called composite scores). Line scores decide which jobs you qualify for.

::: key The single most important fact in this book
Your verbal score (Word Knowledge + Paragraph Comprehension) counts **double** in the AFQT formula. Verbal skills are about **half** of your AFQT. Math (Arithmetic Reasoning + Mathematics Knowledge) is the other half. If you want a 70–80+, these four subtests deserve most of your study time.
:::

## How to Work Through a Chapter

<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Read the lesson</strong> with a pencil in hand. Do the worked examples yourself before reading the solution.</li>
<li><strong>Take the practice set</strong> at the end of the chapter without looking at the answers. Time yourself.</li>
<li><strong>Check every answer</strong>, including the ones you got right. Read the explanation — it often shows a faster method.</li>
<li><strong>Write down every miss</strong> in your error log (Part V has a template). Write <em>why</em> you missed it: did not know the fact, misread the question, or made a careless mistake.</li>
<li><strong>Redo your misses</strong> two or three days later. If you get them right without help, you have truly learned them.</li>
</ol>

## If You Are Short on Time

If your test is close, study in this order:

1. **Chapter 1–3** (how the test works and strategy) — about one hour, and worth it.
2. **Word Knowledge** word parts and the high-frequency word list.
3. **Math Foundations** and **Arithmetic Reasoning** — the four-step method and the common problem types.
4. **Mathematics Knowledge** — algebra basics and the geometry formulas.
5. **Paragraph Comprehension** strategy.
6. One full practice test, then review every miss.
7. Then the technical chapters that matter for the job you want.

## A Note About Accuracy and the "Latest" Test

The facts about the test in this book — the subtests, number of questions, time limits, scoring, retest rules, and the PiCAT — come from the official ASVAB program website (officialasvab.com) and the official recruiting websites of the services, as checked in **October 2026**. Rules such as minimum scores can change when the services change their recruiting needs, so always confirm the current numbers with your recruiter.

All practice questions in this book are original. Real ASVAB questions are controlled test material that no study guide may legally copy or share, and you should never accept or pass along information about actual test questions. The practice questions here are written to match the **style, subjects, and difficulty range** of the real test, so if you can handle them, you are ready.

::: strategy A promise worth making to yourself
Study a little every day instead of a lot once a week. Forty-five focused minutes a day for six to eight weeks will move your score far more than a weekend of cramming. The people who raise their AFQT by 20 or 30 points are almost always the ones who were consistent.
:::
