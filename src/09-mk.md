# Mathematics Knowledge {data-sub="Algebra, geometry, and coordinate rules — plus every formula you need to know by heart"}

Mathematics Knowledge (MK) asks math questions directly — no stories. "Solve for x." "Find the area." "Which expression is equivalent?" It is a quarter of your AFQT and shows up in many line scores (electronics, clerical, skilled technical, and more).

On the computer test you get **31 minutes for 15 questions**, about two minutes each. There is **no formula sheet**, so the formulas in this chapter must live in your head. The good news: MK covers a limited set of topics, and the same ideas come up again and again.

## Algebra Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Variable** | A letter that stands for a number | x, y, n |
| **Coefficient** | The number multiplied by a variable | In 7x, the coefficient is 7 |
| **Constant** | A number by itself | In 7x + 3, the constant is 3 |
| **Term** | A number, a variable, or their product | 7x, 3, −2y^2 |
| **Like terms** | Terms with exactly the same variable part | 4x and −9x are like terms; 4x and 4x^2 are not |
| **Expression** | A math phrase with no equals sign | 3x + 5 |
| **Equation** | Two expressions set equal | 3x + 5 = 20 |
| **Polynomial** | A sum of terms; *monomial* = 1 term, *binomial* = 2, *trinomial* = 3 | x^2 + 5x + 6 is a trinomial |
{.compact}

## Simplifying and Evaluating Expressions

**Combine like terms** by adding their coefficients:
3x + 5y − x + 2y = (3 − 1)x + (5 + 2)y = **2x + 7y**

**Distribute** a number across parentheses — every term gets multiplied:
4(2x − 3) = **8x − 12**   and   −2(x − 5) = **−2x + 10**

::: trap Distributing a negative
A minus sign in front of parentheses changes the sign of **every** term inside: −(3x − 7) = −3x + 7. Forgetting to flip the second sign is one of the most common algebra mistakes.
:::

**Evaluate** by substituting the value — in parentheses — and following the order of operations:

If x = −3, then 2x^2 − 5x + 1 = 2(−3)^2 − 5(−3) + 1 = 2(9) + 15 + 1 = **34**.

## Solving Linear Equations

The goal is to get the variable alone. Whatever you do to one side, do to the other side. Undo operations in reverse order: first addition and subtraction, then multiplication and division.

::: example Examples
**One step at a time:** 3x + 7 = 31 → subtract 7: 3x = 24 → divide by 3: **x = 8**

**Variables on both sides:** 5(x − 2) = 3x + 4 → 5x − 10 = 3x + 4 → 2x − 10 = 4 → 2x = 14 → **x = 7**

**Fractions — clear them first:** [[x/3]] + [[x/6]] = 5. Multiply every term by 6 (the LCD): 2x + x = 30 → 3x = 30 → **x = 10**
:::

::: tip Always check by plugging in
After solving 5(x − 2) = 3x + 4, test x = 7: left side 5(5) = 25; right side 21 + 4 = 25. ✓ It takes ten seconds and catches most errors. If you are stuck, you can also **test the answer choices** this way.
:::

### Solving for one variable in a formula

Treat the other letters like numbers.

- A = lw, solve for w: **w = [[A/l]]**
- P = 2l + 2w, solve for l: P − 2w = 2l → **l = [[(P − 2w)/2]]**
- D = rt, solve for t: **t = [[D/r]]**

## Inequalities

Solve inequalities exactly like equations, with **one extra rule**:

::: key Flip the sign when you multiply or divide by a negative
−2x + 5 > 11 → −2x > 6 → divide by −2 **and flip**: **x < −3**
Check with a number: x = −4 gives 8 + 5 = 13 > 11. ✓
:::

| Symbol | Meaning | On a number line |
|---|---|---|
| < | less than | open circle, arrow left |
| > | greater than | open circle, arrow right |
| ≤ | less than or equal to | closed (filled) circle, arrow left |
| ≥ | greater than or equal to | closed (filled) circle, arrow right |
{.compact}

**Compound inequalities:** do the same thing to all three parts. −1 < 2x + 3 ≤ 9 → −4 < 2x ≤ 6 → **−2 < x ≤ 3**.

## Absolute Value Equations

&#124;x − 4&#124; = 6 means x − 4 is 6 units from zero, so it equals **6 or −6**:
x − 4 = 6 → x = 10, or x − 4 = −6 → x = −2. **Two answers: 10 and −2.**

## Systems of Two Equations

**Substitution** — use when one equation already says "y = ..." or "x = ...":
y = 2x + 1 and 3x + y = 16 → 3x + (2x + 1) = 16 → 5x = 15 → **x = 3**, then y = 2(3) + 1 = **7**.

**Elimination** — use when adding or subtracting the equations cancels a variable:
x + y = 10 and x − y = 4 → add them: 2x = 14 → **x = 7**, then y = **3**.

## Exponents with Variables

The exponent rules from Chapter 7 work the same way with letters:

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Multiply: add exponents | x^3 · x^4 = x^7 |
| Divide: subtract exponents | x^8 ÷ x^2 = x^6 |
| Power of a power: multiply exponents | (x^3)^2 = x^6 |
| Power of a product: apply to every factor | (2x^2y)^3 = 2^3 · x^6 · y^3 = 8x^6y^3 |
| Zero exponent | x^0 = 1 |
| Negative exponent | x^{−2} = [[1/x^2]] |
{.compact}

::: trap Only multiply exponents for a power of a power
x^3 · x^4 is x^7 (add), not x^12. And you can only combine exponents when the **bases are the same**: x^2 · y^3 stays x^2y^3.
:::

## Polynomials

**Adding and subtracting:** combine like terms. When subtracting, distribute the minus sign first:
(3x^2 + 2x − 5) − (x^2 − 4x + 1) = 3x^2 + 2x − 5 − x^2 + 4x − 1 = **2x^2 + 6x − 6**

**Multiplying a monomial by a polynomial:** distribute.
3x(x^2 − 2x + 4) = **3x^3 − 6x^2 + 12x**

**Multiplying two binomials — FOIL:** First, Outer, Inner, Last.

::: example (x + 3)(x − 5)
- **F**irst: x · x = x^2
- **O**uter: x · (−5) = −5x
- **I**nner: 3 · x = 3x
- **L**ast: 3 · (−5) = −15

Combine: x^2 − 5x + 3x − 15 = **x^2 − 2x − 15**
:::

::: formula Special products worth memorizing
- (a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2
- (a − b)^2 = a^2 − 2ab + b^2
- (a + b)(a − b) = a^2 − b^2
:::

::: trap (a + b)^2 is NOT a^2 + b^2
(x + 3)^2 = x^2 + 6x + 9, not x^2 + 9. Write it as (x + 3)(x + 3) and FOIL if you are unsure.
:::

## Factoring

Factoring is FOIL in reverse: turning a polynomial back into a product.

**1. Take out the greatest common factor first:** 6x^2 + 9x = **3x(2x + 3)**

**2. Difference of squares:** a^2 − b^2 = (a + b)(a − b). So x^2 − 49 = **(x + 7)(x − 7)**.

**3. Trinomials x^2 + bx + c:** find two numbers that **multiply to c** and **add to b**.

- x^2 + 7x + 12: numbers that multiply to 12 and add to 7 are 3 and 4 → **(x + 3)(x + 4)**
- x^2 − x − 20: multiply to −20, add to −1 → −5 and 4 → **(x − 5)(x + 4)**
- x^2 − 9x + 20: multiply to 20, add to −9 → −4 and −5 → **(x − 4)(x − 5)**

**4. When the first coefficient is not 1:** try combinations and check with FOIL. 2x^2 + 7x + 3 = **(2x + 1)(x + 3)**. Check: 2x^2 + 6x + x + 3 = 2x^2 + 7x + 3. ✓

**Simplifying fractions with polynomials:** factor, then cancel matching factors.
[[(x^2 − 16)/(x − 4)]] = [[((x + 4)(x − 4))/(x − 4)]] = **x + 4**

## Quadratic Equations

A quadratic equation has an x^2 term. It usually has **two** solutions.

**Method 1 – Factor and use the zero-product rule.** Get 0 on one side, factor, and set each factor equal to 0.
x^2 − 5x + 6 = 0 → (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0 → **x = 2 or x = 3**

**Method 2 – Square roots.** When there is no x term:
x^2 = 49 → **x = 7 or x = −7** (written x = ±7)
(x − 3)^2 = 16 → x − 3 = ±4 → **x = 7 or x = −1**

**Method 3 – The quadratic formula** works for any ax^2 + bx + c = 0:

::: formula The quadratic formula
x = [[−b ± √(b^2 − 4ac)/2a]]

Example: x^2 + 2x − 8 = 0 has a = 1, b = 2, c = −8.
x = [[−2 ± √(4 + 32)/2]] = [[−2 ± 6/2]] → **x = 2 or x = −4**
:::

## Functions

A function is a rule that turns an input into an output. f(x) = 3x − 2 means "multiply by 3, then subtract 2."
f(4) = 3(4) − 2 = **10**. f(−1) = 3(−1) − 2 = **−5**.

## Angles

{{fig:mk-angles|The four basic kinds of angles}}

| Relationship | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Complementary** angles | add up to **90°** |
| **Supplementary** angles | add up to **180°** |
| Angles on a straight line | add up to **180°** |
| Angles around a point | add up to **360°** |
| **Vertical angles** (opposite each other where two lines cross) | are **equal** |
{.compact}

::: memory Complementary vs. supplementary
**C** comes before **S** in the alphabet, and 90 comes before 180. **C**omplementary = 90° (think of a **C**orner). **S**upplementary = 180° (think of a **S**traight line).
:::

### Parallel lines cut by a transversal

When a line (the *transversal*) crosses two parallel lines, it creates eight angles — but only **two different sizes**. Every acute angle is equal to every other acute angle, and every obtuse angle is equal to every other obtuse angle. Any acute angle plus any obtuse angle = 180°.

{{fig:mk-parallel|Angles 1, 4, 5, and 8 are equal; angles 2, 3, 6, and 7 are equal.}}

- **Corresponding angles** (same position at each intersection, like 1 and 5) are equal.
- **Alternate interior angles** (between the parallel lines, on opposite sides of the transversal, like 3 and 6, or 4 and 5) are equal.
- **Alternate exterior angles** (outside the parallel lines, on opposite sides, like 1 and 8, or 2 and 7) are equal.
- **Same-side interior angles** (like 3 and 5, or 4 and 6) add up to 180°.

## Triangles

::: key Triangle facts
- The three angles of every triangle add up to **180°**.
- **Equilateral:** 3 equal sides and 3 equal angles (60° each).
- **Isosceles:** 2 equal sides, and the angles opposite them (the base angles) are equal.
- **Scalene:** no equal sides.
- The **largest angle** is opposite the **longest side**.
- Any side must be **shorter than the sum** of the other two sides.
- An **exterior angle** equals the sum of the two interior angles that are not next to it.
:::

**Area of a triangle = [[1/2]] × base × height**, where the height is measured **straight up and down** (perpendicular) from the base to the opposite corner.

### The Pythagorean theorem

{{fig:mk-pythag|In a right triangle, the hypotenuse c is the side across from the right angle.}}

::: formula The Pythagorean theorem (right triangles only)
**a^2 + b^2 = c^2**, where c is the hypotenuse (the longest side, across from the 90° angle).

Legs 6 and 8: 36 + 64 = 100, so c = √100 = **10**.
Hypotenuse 13 and one leg 5: 25 + b^2 = 169 → b^2 = 144 → b = **12**.
:::

::: tip Memorize the Pythagorean triples
**3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25** — and their multiples (6-8-10, 9-12-15, 12-16-20, 10-24-26). When you see two of the numbers, you know the third without any calculation.
:::

### Special right triangles

{{fig:mk-special|The two special right triangles and their side ratios}}

- **45°-45°-90°:** sides in the ratio **x : x : x√2**. A square's diagonal makes this triangle, so a square with side 5 has a diagonal of 5√2.
- **30°-60°-90°:** sides in the ratio **x : x√3 : 2x**. The shortest side is opposite the 30° angle, and the hypotenuse is **twice** the shortest side.

### Similar triangles

Triangles with the same angles are **similar**: same shape, different size. Their matching sides are **proportional**, which is why shadow and map problems work.

## Quadrilaterals and Other Polygons

| Shape | Perimeter | Area |
|---|---|---|
| **Square** (side s) | 4s | s^2 |
| **Rectangle** (length l, width w) | 2l + 2w | l × w |
| **Parallelogram** (base b, height h) | add the sides | b × h |
| **Trapezoid** (parallel bases b~{1} and b~{2}, height h) | add the sides | [[1/2]] × (b~{1} + b~{2}) × h |
| **Triangle** (base b, height h) | add the sides | [[1/2]] × b × h |
{.compact}

::: formula Angles in any polygon
**Sum of the interior angles = (n − 2) × 180°**, where n is the number of sides.
Each angle of a **regular** polygon (all sides and angles equal) = [[((n − 2) × 180°)/n]].
:::

| Polygon | Sides | Sum of interior angles | Each angle if regular |
|---|---|---|---|
| Triangle | 3 | 180° | 60° |
| Quadrilateral | 4 | 360° | 90° |
| Pentagon | 5 | 540° | 108° |
| Hexagon | 6 | 720° | 120° |
| Octagon | 8 | 1,080° | 135° |
| Decagon | 10 | 1,440° | 144° |
{.compact}

## Circles

{{fig:mk-circle|Parts of a circle}}

::: formula Circle formulas
- **Diameter = 2 × radius** (d = 2r)
- **Circumference = π × d = 2πr** (the distance around)
- **Area = π × r^2**
- A full circle has **360°**. A sector or arc is that fraction of the whole: a 90° sector is [[1/4]] of the circle.
- π ≈ **3.14** or [[22/7]]. Many answers are written "in terms of π," such as 25π.
:::

::: trap Radius or diameter?
Check which one the question gives you. If the diameter is 10, the radius is 5, and the area is π × 5^2 = 25π — **not** 100π.
:::

## Solid Figures: Volume and Surface Area

{{fig:mk-solids|Common solid figures}}

| Solid | Volume | Surface area |
|---|---|---|
| **Cube** (edge s) | s^3 | 6s^2 |
| **Rectangular box** (l, w, h) | l × w × h | 2lw + 2lh + 2wh |
| **Cylinder** (radius r, height h) | πr^2h | 2πr^2 + 2πrh |
| **Cone** (radius r, height h) | [[1/3]]πr^2h | — |
| **Pyramid** (base area B, height h) | [[1/3]]Bh | — |
| **Sphere** (radius r) | [[4/3]]πr^3 | 4πr^2 |
{.compact}

::: key Scaling rule
If you multiply every length of a shape by k, its **area** is multiplied by **k^2** and its **volume** by **k^3**. Double the edge of a cube and its volume becomes 2^3 = **8 times** as large.
:::

## Coordinate Geometry

{{fig:mk-coord|The coordinate plane: points are written (x, y)}}

- A point is written **(x, y)**: x tells how far left or right, y tells how far up or down.
- The four **quadrants** are numbered counterclockwise from the upper right: I (+, +), II (−, +), III (−, −), IV (+, −).

::: formula Coordinate formulas
- **Slope** m = [[(y~{2} − y~{1})/(x~{2} − x~{1})]] = [["rise"/"run"]]
- **Slope-intercept form:** y = mx + b (m = slope, b = y-intercept, where the line crosses the y-axis)
- **Distance** between two points = √((x~{2} − x~{1})^2 + (y~{2} − y~{1})^2)
- **Midpoint** = ([[(x~{1} + x~{2})/2]], [[(y~{1} + y~{2})/2]])
:::

| Slope | What the line does |
|---|---|
| Positive | rises from left to right |
| Negative | falls from left to right |
| Zero | horizontal (y = a number) |
| Undefined | vertical (x = a number) |
{.compact}

- **Parallel lines** have the **same slope**.
- **Perpendicular lines** have slopes that are **negative reciprocals**: if one slope is [[2/3]], the other is −[[3/2]].

::: example Examples
- Slope through (2, 3) and (6, 11): [[(11 − 3)/(6 − 2)]] = [[8/4]] = **2**
- Distance from (1, 2) to (7, 10): √(6^2 + 8^2) = √100 = **10**
- Midpoint of (−4, 7) and (6, −1): ([[2/2]], [[6/2]]) = **(1, 3)**
:::
