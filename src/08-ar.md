# Arithmetic Reasoning {data-sub="Turn word problems into math with a four-step method and a playbook of the problem types that show up again and again"}

Arithmetic Reasoning (AR) is a quarter of your AFQT all by itself, and it appears in almost every line score (it is half of the Army's famous GT score). AR questions are **word problems**: everyday situations — pay, travel, supplies, schedules — that you turn into math.

On the computer test you get **55 minutes for 15 questions**. That is more than three and a half minutes per question, so this subtest rewards careful, organized work far more than speed. Use your scratch paper for every problem.

## The Four-Step Method

<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Find the question.</strong> Read the last sentence first. What exactly are you asked to find, and in what units?</li>
<li><strong>Pull out the facts.</strong> Write down the numbers and what each one means. Cross out information you do not need.</li>
<li><strong>Set it up.</strong> Translate the words into math: an equation, a proportion, or a formula.</li>
<li><strong>Solve and check.</strong> Do the arithmetic, then ask: Does the answer make sense? Is it the thing the question asked for?</li>
</ol>

::: example Example: the four steps in action
A recruiter drives 45 miles to one high school, 63 miles to a second school, and then 72 miles back to the office. The car gets 24 miles per gallon, and gas costs $3.60 per gallon. How much did the gas for the trip cost?

1. **Question:** the cost of gas, in dollars.
2. **Facts:** 45 mi, 63 mi, 72 mi; 24 miles per gallon; $3.60 per gallon.
3. **Set up:** total miles → gallons used → cost.
4. **Solve:** 45 + 63 + 72 = 180 miles. 180 ÷ 24 = 7.5 gallons. 7.5 × $3.60 = **$27.00**.
   **Check:** about 8 gallons at about $3.50 is about $28. ✓
:::

## Translating Words into Math

| When you see... | Write... | Example |
|---|---|---|
| sum, total, combined, plus, more than, increased by, added to | **+** | "7 more than x" → x + 7 |
| difference, minus, less than, fewer, decreased by, left, remain | **−** | "7 less than x" → x − 7 |
| product, times, of, twice, double, triple | **×** | "twice x" → 2x; "half of x" → [[1/2]]x |
| quotient, divided by, per, ratio, split equally, average | **÷** | "x split among 4" → x ÷ 4 |
| is, was, equals, will be, gives, results in | **=** | "x is 12" → x = 12 |
| what, a number, how many, how much | a letter such as **x** | "what number" → x |
| percent | **÷ 100** | "15% of x" → 0.15x |
{.compact}

::: trap "Less than" flips the order
"5 less than a number" is **x − 5**, not 5 − x. Read it as "start with the number, then take 5 away." The same goes for "subtracted from": "3 subtracted from y" is y − 3.
:::

## The Problem-Type Playbook

Almost every AR question belongs to one of the types below. When you can name the type, you already know the method.

### 1. Multi-step totals and "how many are left"

::: example Example
A unit orders 18 cases of water with 24 bottles in each case. During a training exercise, 137 bottles are used. How many bottles are left?

Total bottles: 18 × 24 = 432. Left: 432 − 137 = **295 bottles**.
:::

::: tip Round up when you need "enough"
If each box holds 24 meals and you need 350 meals, 350 ÷ 24 = 14.6 boxes — but you cannot order 0.6 of a box. You need **15** boxes. Questions about buses, boxes, trips, and containers usually need rounding **up**.
:::

### 2. Percent problems: discounts, tax, tips, commission

| Situation | Method |
|---|---|
| Sale price after a d% discount | price × (1 − d) — for 25% off, multiply by 0.75 |
| Total with t% tax | price × (1 + t) — for 6% tax, multiply by 1.06 |
| Commission of c% | sales × c |
| Original price before a d% discount | sale price ÷ (1 − d) |
{.compact}

::: example Example: discount, then tax
A pair of boots costs $240. It is on sale for 25% off, and sales tax of 6% is charged on the sale price. What is the total cost?

Sale price: $240 × 0.75 = $180. Tax: $180 × 0.06 = $10.80. Total: $180 + $10.80 = **$190.80**.
:::

::: trap Working backward from a sale price
"After a 20% discount, a jacket costs $64. What was the original price?" The tempting answer is $64 + 20% of $64 = $76.80. **Wrong** — the 20% was taken from the *original* price, not from $64.
Correct: $64 is 80% of the original, so the original = $64 ÷ 0.80 = **$80**. Check: 20% of $80 is $16, and $80 − $16 = $64. ✓
:::

### 3. Ratio and proportion

Set up two equal fractions with **matching units in the same positions**, then cross-multiply.

::: example Examples
**Recipe:** 3 cups of flour make 24 biscuits. How much flour for 40 biscuits?
[[3/24]] = [[x/40]] → 24x = 120 → **x = 5 cups**.

**Shadows:** A 6-foot soldier casts a 4-foot shadow. At the same time, a flagpole casts a 22-foot shadow. How tall is the flagpole?
[[6/4]] = [[x/22]] → 4x = 132 → **x = 33 feet**.
:::

### 4. Distance, rate, and time

::: formula D = R × T
**Distance = Rate × Time.** Rearranged: Rate = Distance ÷ Time, and Time = Distance ÷ Rate.
Keep units consistent: miles per **hour** goes with time in **hours**. 30 minutes = 0.5 hour; 20 minutes = [[1/3]] hour; 45 minutes = 0.75 hour.
:::

| Situation | What to do with the speeds |
|---|---|
| Two travelers moving **apart** (opposite directions) | **Add** the speeds |
| Two travelers moving **toward each other** | **Add** the speeds |
| One traveler **catching up** to another | **Subtract** the speeds |
{.compact}

::: example Example: moving apart
Two trucks leave the same depot at the same time, traveling in opposite directions at 45 mph and 55 mph. After how many hours will they be 350 miles apart?
Together they separate at 45 + 55 = 100 mph. Time = 350 ÷ 100 = **3.5 hours**.
:::

::: trap Average speed is NOT the average of the speeds
You drive 120 miles to a range at 60 mph and return the same 120 miles at 40 mph. What is your average speed?
It is tempting to say 50 mph. But time there = 2 hours and time back = 3 hours. Average speed = **total distance ÷ total time** = 240 ÷ 5 = **48 mph**.
:::

### 5. Work problems (people or machines working together)

Think in terms of **rate = jobs per hour**. If someone finishes a job in 6 hours, they do [[1/6]] of the job each hour. Rates working together **add**.

::: example Example
Pat can paint a room in 6 hours. Sam can paint the same room in 3 hours. How long will it take them working together?
Combined rate: [[1/6]] + [[1/3]] = [[1/6]] + [[2/6]] = [[3/6]] = [[1/2]] room per hour. So the job takes **2 hours**.
:::

::: tip Shortcut for two workers
Time together = [[(A × B)/(A + B)]]. For 6 and 3 hours: [[18/9]] = 2 hours. The answer is always **less than the faster worker's time** — use that to eliminate choices instantly.
:::

If one thing **fills** and another **drains**, **subtract** the draining rate. A pipe that fills a tank in 3 hours with a drain that empties it in 6 hours: [[1/3]] − [[1/6]] = [[1/6]] per hour → **6 hours** to fill.

When a job is measured in **worker-days**, multiply: if 6 workers take 10 days, the job is 60 worker-days, so 15 workers need 60 ÷ 15 = **4 days**.

### 6. Interest

::: formula Simple interest
**I = P × r × t**
Interest = Principal (amount borrowed or saved) × rate (as a decimal) × time (in **years**).
:::

::: example Examples
- $2,000 saved at 4% simple interest for 3 years: I = 2,000 × 0.04 × 3 = **$240**. The account then holds $2,240.
- For 6 months, use t = 0.5 year.
- **Compound interest** earns interest on interest. $1,000 at 10% compounded once a year for 2 years: after year 1, $1,100; after year 2, $1,100 × 1.10 = **$1,210**.
:::

### 7. Averages

Average = sum ÷ number of items. Work with **totals** whenever an average problem looks tricky.

::: example Weighted average
In one class, 10 recruits average 20 push-ups and 30 recruits average 40 push-ups. What is the average for all 40 recruits?
Total push-ups = 10 × 20 + 30 × 40 = 200 + 1,200 = 1,400. Average = 1,400 ÷ 40 = **35** (not 30 — the larger group pulls the average toward 40).
:::

### 8. Money, unit price, and pay

- **Best buy:** divide price by quantity to get the **unit price** and compare. 12 oz for $3.00 is $0.25 per ounce; 20 oz for $4.40 is $0.22 per ounce — the 20-ounce size is the better buy.
- **Overtime:** "time-and-a-half" means 1.5 × the regular hourly rate for hours over 40. At $18 per hour, 46 hours pays 40 × $18 + 6 × $27 = $720 + $162 = **$882**.

::: example Coin problem
Thirty coins, all dimes and quarters, are worth $4.80. How many are quarters?
Let q = quarters, so dimes = 30 − q. Value: 0.25q + 0.10(30 − q) = 4.80 → 0.25q + 3 − 0.10q = 4.80 → 0.15q = 1.80 → **q = 12**.
Shortcut: **test the answer choices** — 12 quarters ($3.00) plus 18 dimes ($1.80) = $4.80. ✓
:::

### 9. Age problems

Use a letter for one person's age **now**, write everyone else's age in terms of it, and remember that **everyone ages the same number of years**.

::: example Example
A father is 3 times as old as his son. In 12 years, he will be twice as old as his son. How old is the son now?
Now: son = s, father = 3s. In 12 years: son = s + 12, father = 3s + 12.
3s + 12 = 2(s + 12) → 3s + 12 = 2s + 24 → **s = 12**. (The father is 36. In 12 years: 48 and 24. ✓)
:::

### 10. Number problems and consecutive integers

- Consecutive integers: x, x + 1, x + 2, ...
- Consecutive **even** or **odd** integers: x, x + 2, x + 4, ...

::: example Example
The sum of three consecutive integers is 72. What are they?
x + (x + 1) + (x + 2) = 72 → 3x + 3 = 72 → 3x = 69 → x = 23. The integers are **23, 24, and 25**.
Shortcut: the middle number is the average, 72 ÷ 3 = 24.
:::

### 11. Mixture problems

Multiply each amount by its strength (or price), and set the totals equal.

::: example Example
A 30-pound mix of peanuts ($2 per pound) and cashews ($5 per pound) sells for $3 per pound. How many pounds of cashews are in it?
Let c = pounds of cashews; peanuts = 30 − c.
5c + 2(30 − c) = 3 × 30 → 5c + 60 − 2c = 90 → 3c = 30 → **c = 10 pounds**.
:::

### 12. Measurement and geometry word problems

| Situation | What to use |
|---|---|
| Fencing, trim, framing, "distance around" | **Perimeter** (add the sides) |
| Carpet, tile, paint, sod, "cover" | **Area** (length × width for rectangles) |
| Fill, hold, pour, "capacity" | **Volume** (length × width × height for boxes) |
| One trip around a wheel or circular track | **Circumference** (π × diameter) |
{.compact}

::: trap Square feet vs. square yards
1 yard = 3 feet, but 1 **square** yard = 3 × 3 = **9 square feet**, and 1 **cubic** yard = 27 cubic feet. A 12-by-18-foot room is 216 square feet = 216 ÷ 9 = **24 square yards**.
:::

Useful facts: 1 cubic foot of water ≈ 7.5 gallons; π ≈ 3.14 or [[22/7]].

### 13. Probability and counting

::: formula Probability
**Probability = (number of favorable outcomes) ÷ (total number of outcomes)**
It is always between 0 (impossible) and 1 (certain).
:::

- A bag holds 4 red and 6 blue marbles. P(red) = [[4/10]] = [[2/5]].
- **"And" for independent events:** multiply. Two coin flips both landing heads: [[1/2]] × [[1/2]] = [[1/4]].
- **Without replacement:** the second draw has one fewer item. Drawing 2 red marbles from the bag above: [[4/10]] × [[3/9]] = [[12/90]] = [[2/15]].
- **Counting choices:** multiply the number of options at each step. 3 shirts × 4 pairs of pants = 12 outfits.
- **Handshakes (pairs from a group of n):** n × (n − 1) ÷ 2. Six people → 6 × 5 ÷ 2 = 15 handshakes.

### 14. Time and schedules

- **Elapsed time:** count up in easy chunks. 0945 to 1520: 0945 → 1445 is 5 hours; 1445 → 1520 is 35 minutes. Total **5 hours 35 minutes**.
- **Events that repeat:** use the **LCM**. A guard changes every 4 hours and a patrol leaves every 6 hours; if both happen at 0600, they happen together again every 12 hours — next at **1800**.

## Checking Your Answer

Before you lock in an answer, run through this quick list:

- **Size check:** Is it reasonable? A sale price cannot exceed the original price; a team working together cannot be slower than its fastest member.
- **Units check:** Did the question ask for minutes or hours? Feet or yards? Dollars or cents?
- **Question check:** Did you find the *final* answer, or a middle step? (The discount instead of the sale price is a classic slip.)
- **Plug-in check:** Put your answer back into the story. Does everything work?
