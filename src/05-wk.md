# Word Knowledge {data-sub="Build your vocabulary fast with word parts, context clues, and the words that show up most"}

Word Knowledge (WK) tests one thing: **do you know what words mean?** It is the fastest subtest on the ASVAB — about 36 seconds per question on the computer version — and it is half of your verbal score, which counts double in the AFQT. Every word you learn is valuable.

The good news: you do not need to memorize the dictionary. Most test words are built from a small set of **word parts**, and many questions give you a **sentence** that hints at the meaning. This chapter teaches you both skills and then gives you a list of high-value words to learn.

## What WK Questions Look Like

There are two formats.

**Format 1 – The word by itself**

> **Abundant** most nearly means:
> A) scarce  B) plentiful  C) expensive  D) hidden

**Format 2 – The word in a sentence**

> The medic was **meticulous** in recording every detail of the patient's condition.
> *Meticulous* most nearly means:
> A) careless  B) quick  C) extremely careful  D) nervous

In both formats you are looking for the choice that is the **closest synonym** — the word that could replace the test word without changing the meaning. The answers are **plentiful** and **extremely careful**.

## The Six-Step Method

<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Read the word (and the sentence, if there is one).</strong></li>
<li><strong>Define it yourself first.</strong> Before looking at the choices, say what you think it means. Even a rough idea ("it's something negative," "it has to do with time") helps.</li>
<li><strong>If there is a sentence, cover the word and predict.</strong> Read the sentence with a blank in place of the word. What word would make sense there?</li>
<li><strong>Test each choice in the sentence.</strong> Replace the test word with each answer. The right one keeps the meaning of the sentence.</li>
<li><strong>Use word parts and "charge."</strong> Break the word into prefix, root, and suffix. Decide whether it feels positive, negative, or neutral.</li>
<li><strong>Eliminate and choose.</strong> Cross out choices with the wrong charge, the wrong part of speech, or ones that only <em>sound</em> like the test word.</li>
</ol>

::: trap Sound-alike traps
Test writers love answer choices that **look or sound like** the test word but mean something different. *Lethargic* (sluggish) is not *lethal* (deadly). *Eminent* (famous) is not *imminent* (about to happen). *Candid* (honest) has nothing to do with candy. If a choice looks too similar to the test word, be suspicious.
:::

::: tip Positive, negative, or neutral?
Even when you cannot define a word, you can often feel its **charge**. *Malicious, belligerent,* and *callous* all feel negative. *Benevolent, amiable,* and *jovial* feel positive. If the test word feels negative, eliminate the positive choices — you are already down to two or three options.
:::

## Context Clues: Let the Sentence Teach You

When a word appears in a sentence, the sentence usually contains a hint. There are five common kinds of clues.

| Clue type | What to look for | Example (the clue is in italics) |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | The sentence explains the word, often after a comma, dash, or "or" | The general admired his aide's **candor**, *or honesty*, in every briefing. |
| **Synonym** | A word with a similar meaning nearby | The recruit was **diligent**; she was *as hardworking as anyone* in the platoon. |
| **Contrast** | Words like *but, unlike, although, however, instead* signal an opposite | *Unlike* his talkative brother, Marcus was **taciturn**. (taciturn = quiet) |
| **Example** | A list of examples shows the meaning | **Inclement** weather — *rain, sleet, and high winds* — delayed the exercise. (inclement = stormy, harsh) |
| **General sense** | The logic of the whole sentence | After *marching for twelve hours*, the soldiers were **weary**. (weary = very tired) |
{.compact}

::: example Example: using a contrast clue
Although the first sergeant seemed **gruff**, he was actually kind to new soldiers.
*Gruff* most nearly means:
A) generous  B) rough and unfriendly  C) elderly  D) tall

**Think:** *Although ... actually kind* signals a contrast. So *gruff* must be the opposite of kind. The answer is **B, rough and unfriendly**.
:::

## Word Parts: The Master Key

Most English words that appear on the ASVAB come from Latin and Greek parts. A word can have up to three kinds of parts:

- A **prefix** at the beginning changes the meaning: **un**happy, **pre**view.
- A **root** in the middle carries the core meaning: in**spect**, **port**able.
- A **suffix** at the end often changes the part of speech: kind**ness** (noun), hope**ful** (adjective).

::: example Example: decoding a word you have never seen
**Circumnavigate** = *circum* (around) + *navig* (sail, steer) + *-ate* (to do)
= **to sail all the way around.**

**Incredulous** = *in* (not) + *cred* (believe) + *-ous* (full of)
= **full of not-believing — unable or unwilling to believe.**
:::

::: tip Part of speech is a free clue
The answer to a WK question is almost always the **same part of speech** as the test word. If the test word ends in *-ous* (an adjective, like *perilous*), the answer will be an adjective too (*dangerous*). If it ends in *-ate* (*alleviate*), look for a verb (*relieve*).
:::

## Words With More Than One Meaning

Some words have several meanings. When the word is in a sentence, the sentence decides which one is being tested.

| Word | Meaning 1 | Meaning 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **sound** | a noise | healthy or reliable ("sound judgment") |
| **fast** | quick | firmly fixed ("hold fast"); to go without food |
| **grave** | a burial place | serious ("a grave mistake") |
| **novel** | a long story | new and original ("a novel idea") |
| **sanction** | official approval | a penalty ("trade sanctions") |
| **compound** | made of parts; a mixture | an enclosed area ("a fenced compound") |
| **stern** | strict and serious | the back of a ship |
| **mean** | unkind | average; to intend |
| **fair** | just, honest | light in color; a carnival |
| **bolt** | a metal fastener | to run off suddenly |
{.compact}

|---|---|---|---|

| **complement** | something that completes | **compliment** | praise |
| **conscience** | sense of right and wrong | **conscious** | awake, aware |
| **discreet** | careful, tactful | **discrete** | separate, distinct |
| **elicit** | to draw out | **illicit** | illegal |
| **eminent** | famous, respected | **imminent** | about to happen |
| **emigrate** | to leave a country | **immigrate** | to come into a country |
| **explicit** | stated clearly | **implicit** | implied, unstated |
| **imply** | to suggest | **infer** | to conclude from clues |
| **negligent** | careless | **negligible** | too small to matter |
| **precede** | to come before | **proceed** | to go forward |
| **principal** | main; a school head | **principle** | a rule or belief |
