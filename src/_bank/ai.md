# Auto Information

::: quiz ai2 | Auto Information – Practice Set 2 | group=bank | time=14 minutes
? In a four-stroke gasoline engine, which stroke produces power?
- Intake
- Compression
- Power (combustion) *
- Exhaust
> The spark ignites the fuel-air mix, which pushes the piston down on the **power stroke**. The order is intake, compression, power, exhaust.

? What ignites the fuel-air mixture in a gasoline engine?
- Glow plug
- Spark plug *
- Heat of compression alone
- Starter motor
> A **spark plug** fires to ignite the mixture. Diesel engines ignite fuel with the heat of compression instead.

? Diesel engines ignite their fuel by:
- spark plugs.
- a distributor.
- the heat of highly compressed air. *
- a magneto.
> Diesels squeeze air so hard that it gets hot enough to **ignite the injected fuel** without a spark.

? The part that changes the up-and-down motion of the pistons into rotating motion is the:
- camshaft.
- crankshaft. *
- driveshaft.
- flywheel.
> Connecting rods turn the **crankshaft**, which converts reciprocating motion into rotation.

? The camshaft controls the:
- opening and closing of the valves. *
- fuel pressure.
- battery charging.
- steering angle.
> Lobes on the **camshaft** push the valves open at the right moments.

? What does the radiator do?
- Heats the passenger compartment only
- Removes heat from the engine coolant *
- Filters the engine oil
- Stores extra fuel
> Coolant picks up engine heat, and the **radiator releases that heat** to the air.

? The thermostat in the cooling system:
- pumps coolant through the engine.
- controls coolant flow so the engine reaches and keeps operating temperature. *
- measures oil pressure.
- cools the transmission fluid.
> The **thermostat** stays closed while the engine is cold and opens once it warms up.

? An engine overheats and the coolant level is fine. A likely cause is:
- a stuck-closed thermostat. *
- a weak battery.
- worn wiper blades.
- low tire pressure.
> If the **thermostat sticks closed**, coolant cannot reach the radiator and the engine overheats.

? Which component keeps the battery charged while the engine runs?
- Starter
- Alternator *
- Distributor
- Ignition coil
> The **alternator** generates electricity while the engine runs and recharges the battery.

? A clicking sound but no cranking when the key is turned usually points to:
- a weak battery or bad connection. *
- low oil.
- a bad fuel pump.
- worn brake pads.
> A **weak battery or corroded cable** cannot spin the starter, so the solenoid just clicks.

? The catalytic converter reduces:
- engine noise.
- harmful exhaust emissions. *
- fuel consumption.
- tire wear.
> The **catalytic converter** turns harmful gases into less harmful ones before they leave the tailpipe.

? The muffler reduces:
- exhaust noise. *
- engine heat.
- exhaust emissions.
- oil leaks.
> The **muffler** quiets the exhaust sound.

? Disc brakes stop the wheel by squeezing pads against a:
- drum.
- rotor. *
- caliper bracket.
- master cylinder.
> The caliper squeezes the pads against the **rotor** (disc). Drum brakes push shoes outward against a drum.

? A brake pedal that feels spongy most likely means:
- air in the brake lines. *
- worn tires.
- too much engine oil.
- a loose fan belt.
> **Air in the hydraulic lines** compresses, making the pedal soft. The brakes need bleeding.

? The master cylinder is part of the:
- cooling system.
- hydraulic brake system. *
- fuel system.
- charging system.
> The **master cylinder** turns pedal force into hydraulic pressure for the brakes.

? A squealing noise when braking usually means:
- the brake pads are worn. *
- the radiator is low.
- the alternator is failing.
- the timing is off.
> Many pads have a metal **wear indicator** that squeals when the pad is thin.

? Shock absorbers are part of the:
- suspension system. *
- exhaust system.
- ignition system.
- fuel system.
> **Shocks** dampen spring motion as part of the suspension, keeping tires on the road.

? Uneven wear on the inside or outside edges of the front tires usually means:
- the tires are overinflated.
- the wheels are out of alignment. *
- the engine oil is low.
- the battery is weak.
> Edge wear usually comes from bad **alignment** (camber or toe).

? Wear down the center of a tire's tread usually means the tire is:
- underinflated.
- overinflated. *
- out of balance.
- the wrong size.
> An **overinflated** tire bulges in the middle and wears the center. Underinflation wears both edges.

? The device that lets the drive wheels turn at different speeds in a turn is the:
- clutch.
- differential. *
- universal joint.
- torque converter.
> The **differential** lets the outer wheel travel farther than the inner wheel in a turn.

? In a car with an automatic transmission, the part that connects the engine to the transmission is the:
- clutch disc.
- torque converter. *
- flywheel bolt.
- differential.
> An automatic uses a fluid-filled **torque converter**. A manual uses a clutch.

? Blue-gray smoke from the exhaust usually means the engine is:
- burning oil. *
- running too lean.
- low on coolant.
- in perfect condition.
> **Blue smoke** means oil is getting into the cylinders, often from worn rings or valve seals. White smoke can mean coolant; black smoke means too much fuel.

? Black smoke from a gasoline engine's exhaust usually means:
- burning oil.
- a rich fuel mixture (too much fuel). *
- a coolant leak.
- a lean mixture.
> **Black smoke** is unburned fuel from a rich mixture.

? The fuel injector's job is to:
- filter fuel.
- spray a fine mist of fuel into the engine. *
- store fuel.
- pump fuel from the tank.
> **Injectors** spray a precise mist of fuel for clean, efficient burning.

? The oil pump:
- cools the engine.
- circulates oil to lubricate engine parts. *
- removes water from fuel.
- pressurizes the brakes.
> The **oil pump** pushes oil through passages to the bearings and other moving parts.

? Which gauge warns you right away of a problem that could quickly ruin the engine?
- Fuel gauge
- Oil pressure warning *
- Odometer
- Tachometer
> Losing **oil pressure** can destroy an engine in minutes. Stop the engine right away.

? Antifreeze is added to the cooling system to:
- lower the freezing point and raise the boiling point of the coolant. *
- clean the fuel injectors.
- increase oil pressure.
- charge the battery.
> **Antifreeze** (usually ethylene glycol) protects against freezing and boiling and also helps prevent corrosion.

? A U-joint (universal joint) is found on the:
- driveshaft. *
- radiator.
- spark plug.
- muffler.
> **U-joints** let the driveshaft flex as the rear axle moves up and down.

? The rack-and-pinion assembly is part of the:
- braking system.
- steering system. *
- exhaust system.
- charging system.
> **Rack-and-pinion** turns the steering wheel's rotation into side-to-side motion of the wheels.

? An OBD-II scan tool is used to:
- measure tire tread depth.
- read trouble codes from the vehicle's computer. *
- check coolant strength.
- adjust headlights.
> The **OBD-II** port lets a scanner read diagnostic codes, such as the reason the check engine light is on.
