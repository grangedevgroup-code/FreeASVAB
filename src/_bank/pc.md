[[passage Passage 1]]
Vehicle operators must complete a walk-around inspection before every trip. The inspection includes checking tire pressure, looking for fluid leaks under the vehicle, and testing all lights and signals. Any problem that could affect safe operation must be reported to the motor pool before the vehicle leaves. Minor issues, such as a dirty windshield, should be fixed by the operator on the spot.
[[/passage]]
? According to the passage, when must the walk-around inspection be done?
- Once a week
- After every trip
- Before every trip *
- Only when a problem is suspected
> The first sentence says the inspection happens **before every trip**.

? What should an operator do about a dirty windshield?
- Report it to the motor pool.
- Fix it on the spot. *
- Ignore it until the next service.
- Cancel the trip.
> The passage lists a dirty windshield as a **minor issue the operator fixes on the spot**.

[[passage Passage 2]]
Many people believe that cracking your knuckles causes arthritis. Researchers who studied people who had cracked their knuckles for decades found no greater rate of arthritis than among people who never did. The popping sound comes from gas bubbles forming in the fluid that cushions the joint, not from bones grinding together.
[[/passage]]
? The main idea of the passage is that:
- knuckle cracking causes arthritis.
- knuckle cracking does not appear to cause arthritis. *
- arthritis is caused by gas bubbles.
- people should stop cracking their knuckles.
> The studies found **no link** between knuckle cracking and arthritis.

? According to the passage, the popping sound comes from:
- bones grinding together.
- torn ligaments.
- gas bubbles in joint fluid. *
- muscles tightening.
> The last sentence says the sound comes from **gas bubbles** in the fluid around the joint.

[[passage Passage 4]]
Bamboo is one of the fastest-growing plants on Earth. Some species can grow more than two feet in a single day. Because it regrows quickly after being cut and needs little fertilizer, bamboo is often called a renewable building material. In many parts of Asia it has been used for scaffolding on tall buildings for centuries.
[[/passage]]
? Bamboo is called a renewable building material because it:
- is used in Asia.
- is very strong.
- regrows quickly and needs little fertilizer. *
- can be used for scaffolding.
> The passage gives the reason directly: it **regrows quickly after being cut and needs little fertilizer**.

? In this passage, the word *scaffolding* most nearly means:
- a temporary framework used during construction. *
- a type of roof.
- a decorative fence.
- a kind of furniture.
> Scaffolding on tall buildings is a **temporary framework** workers stand on while building.

[[passage Passage 5]]
A common mistake among new runners is increasing their weekly distance too quickly. Most coaches recommend raising total mileage by no more than about ten percent per week. Runners who ignore this guideline are more likely to develop shin splints and stress fractures, injuries that can keep them from training for weeks.
[[/passage]]
? A runner who ran 20 miles this week should, following the guideline, run no more than about how many miles next week?
- 20
- 22 *
- 25
- 30
> Ten percent of 20 is 2, so the runner should go up to about **22 miles**.

? The author's main purpose is to:
- describe the history of running.
- warn runners about increasing mileage too fast. *
- explain how to treat stress fractures.
- compare different running shoes.
> The passage explains a **common mistake** and its consequences, which is a warning.

[[passage Passage 6]]
Before 1883, each American town set its clocks by the local position of the sun, so noon in one city could be several minutes different from noon in the next. This caused confusion for railroads trying to publish train schedules. To solve the problem, the railroads divided the country into standard time zones, and towns gradually adopted them.
[[/passage]]
? According to the passage, standard time zones were created mainly to:
- save energy.
- help farmers.
- make train schedules less confusing. *
- match European time.
> The railroads created time zones because local times made **train schedules confusing**.

? It can be inferred that before 1883:
- all clocks in the country showed the same time.
- noon in two nearby towns might not happen at the same moment. *
- trains did not run on schedules.
- towns did not use clocks.
> Each town used its own solar noon, so **nearby towns could differ** by a few minutes.
